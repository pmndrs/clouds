# Audit, November 2026: why the clouds don't look right, and the plan

Written 2026-11-21 as an outside review. It ignores the repo's existing rules and phase plans. The evidence is the code, the capture sheets, the reports and the git history.

## The goal

Set by the owner, restated 2026-11-21 so the plan cannot drift from it:

**The best-looking, fastest cloud system that can run in the browser.**

- **Looks are judged against reality**: the owner's photos, simulated clouds (VDBs) the owner rates highly, and physically correct light from a path trace. Then the owner's eye.
- **Speed is judged in milliseconds** on the target machines.
- **Every source is a toolbox, not a target.** Guerrilla's Horizon and Nubis work, Takram, Sky Pro, Frostbite, the papers and Houdini are places to take methods from where they help. None of them is a gate. Horizon's clouds are one style, and even they look a little odd beside real clouds and good VDBs.
- **Diverging is fine when the reason is better quality, shape or speed.** A method is kept because it measures or looks better here, not because a source does it, and not dropped because a source doesn't.
- This is not a licence to skip hard work for a small saving, or to avoid something only because Guerrilla does it. On looks especially, they are a style and a source, never the rule.

How the plan holds to this: no gate anywhere below compares against a Horizon frame, a Decima number or a published constant. Gates compare against the path trace, the converged march, the photo measures, the millisecond targets and the owner's look.

## Verdict

Do not restart. The renderer infrastructure, the atmosphere, the capture harness, the VDB path, the upscaler levels and the path-traced reference page are all worth keeping.

What is wrong is not one bug. The project has never had a fixed thing to be right against, so each round tuned toward a different target and left its constants behind. Three separate problems (shape, light, image reconstruction) were tuned through each other. The plan below separates them and gives each its own ground truth.

Your instinct about the path tracer is right for lighting. It cannot fix shape, and shape is the larger part of what is wrong in the sky views.

## What is going wrong

### 1. Shape: the sky is a 2D map extruded into slabs

Compare `work/captures/S1/final/horizon/ground.jpg` with `artifacts/research/cloud-photos/_targets/texas-afternoon.jpg`.

| | Your photo | Our sky |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body proportions | from flat humilis to a tower taller than it is wide, in one frame | every cloud is a wide, thin pancake of about the same thickness |
| Structure | rounded lobes at three or four sizes, each made of smaller ones | one large outline, then fine fuzz near pixel size, nothing between |
| Bases | flat but soft, darker toward the middle | hard flat undersides with visible contour lines |
| Lit faces | smooth, with shading only at lobe scale | covered edge to edge in fine texture |

The cause is structural. `hzdMainSample` builds a cloud as a 2D coverage value times a height profile, then erodes it with noise (`packages/runtime/src/procedural/hzd/shaper.wgsl.ts:454`). Nothing in that chain makes mid-scale 3D billows, so the erosion noise is the only source of surface structure. That is the fuzz. The reports name this defect seven times under different words (slab, pancake, terrace, shelf, "from above reads flat") and fix it per view each time.

No path tracer will fix this. A perfect renderer would draw these same slabs, correctly lit.

**Corrections after measuring (2026-11-21).** Two parts of this finding did not hold up as written:
- *One body under correct light looks plausible.* A patch of the procedural sky, path traced (step 2.0, `work/captures/card/sky/contact.jpg`), reads as a soft cumulus. At the scale of one body, lighting is the larger fault. The patch was baked at 8 m and is one body seen from the side, so this does not test the sky-wide sameness.
- *"Nothing between" is not in the silhouette.* The photo measures (step 2.1, `work/reports/P2-1-shape-measures.md`) put our mid-scale outline structure inside the photo range. What does separate us from the photos: more fine fuzz on the outline, more fine structure on bases, a larger share of flat down-facing outline, and smaller clear-sky gaps. If a missing middle scale is real, it is in the interior shading, which is lighting.
- *"Same-thickness slabs" is not yet measured.* From below, our sky segments as one connected deck, so body aspect could not be compared. It needs the renderer's own alpha with a body ID.

### 2. Light: measurable errors, and they are ours

PT1 is the best piece of work in the repo. Same density, same sun and sky, and the only difference is the lighting model. It found:

- lit faces carry 1.6 to 1.9 times too much fine texture;
- thin fringes are solid white where they should be see-through;
- backlit bodies are too dark (Nubis 0.81x, octave 0.51x of the reference);
- shade is too blue in one model and too brown in the other.

The foam has a direct cause in the code. `cloudInScatterMicro` multiplies the multiple-scattering terms by `microOcc`, a single-scatter shadow over the first few metres (`packages/runtime/src/shaders/lighting.wgsl.ts:63`). Multiple scattering is exactly the light that does not care about a 5 m pit. That term was added in round Q-1 to make lobes read, then strengthened in D1 to match Decima's florets.

The lighting function now sums six terms (direct, glow, deep octaves, diffuse sun, environment, ambient). Its comments cite nine different rounds, tuned against four different references: Tidewater captures, Decima frames, Karma renders and the path trace. No one can reason about a model built that way, and no single round could have fixed it.

### 3. The target moved six times

Legacy raymarch, Nubis voxel runtime, Takram and Sky Pro stopgap, HZD shaper, Decima parity, then styles. Each left a switchable path behind. Today there are 3 shapers, 2 lighting models, 3 looks, 3 styles, 2 noise sets, 2 map recipes, 23 review switches and about 670 tunable settings. Every A/B happens in a different corner of that space, so results do not carry over.

### 4. The parity target was one you don't like

In the calibration round you scored Guerrilla's Parkouring cloud 4 out of 10 and the simulated VDBs 8, 7 and 7. D1 then spent 12 rounds matching our render of Parkouring to Decima's frames. It plateaued at 3 to 5 out of 10 by the judges and was never signed off. The floret micro-shadow that PT1 identifies as the foam was tuned in those rounds.

### 5. The judges do not see what you see

Rank correlation with your scores was 0.38 for Fable and 0.04 for Opus. Both missed your main axis, material and cohesion. They were still the main measure for 12 more rounds. `typeFromScale` won 11 of 12 judged verdicts and was reversed within a day.

### 6. Three problems tuned through each other

Foam was treated as a noise problem: the detail noise was swapped three times, and two of the three styles now swap it back. PT1 shows most of it is lighting. Blue shade was "fixed" in LH1 by desaturating the ambient, and S1 then found the blue was mostly aerial perspective. Hatch was fixed five times in five places because it is a sampling problem that kept being judged in look rounds.

### 7. Pace outran review

507 of 602 commits landed in eight days. There are 23 switches waiting for your verdict. Agents chose defaults, later agents tuned on top of them, and your eye came in after the fact.

### 8. Speed was parked, and the look is being judged at a cost that cannot ship

The sky pass is 25 to 45 ms at the level where looks are judged. The target is 4 ms. Whatever gets the cost down will change the look again, and there is no measure to say by how much.

### Why Takram and the September look are better

My read, not measured: both use one small, physically motivated lighting model with no terms added to chase a reference, and both erode lightly. PT1 itself notes the September look is closer to the path trace than the current bench. Fewer terms beat more terms here.

### A caution on "path trace as ground truth"

The October audit blamed part of the first failure on a brute-force reference as the acceptance bar. That was different: it made a reference integrator the product's foundation and demanded pixel parity. The use here is narrower. The path tracer is a measuring instrument for lighting only, with tolerance bands, and the realtime renderer is free to take shortcuts inside them.

It also has real gaps today. It has a single HG lobe, was validated only up to g 0.6, has no single/multi scatter split, no ground plane with volumes, and PT1 compared tone-mapped PNGs, not linear radiance.

## The plan

### Rules

1. **Three problems, three truths, never mixed in one round.**
   - *Light*: path trace of the same density. Measured.
   - *Image* (stepping, jitter, temporal, upscale): a converged march of our own lighting, with many steps, no history and supersampling. Measured.
   - *Shape*: your photos and the VDBs you rated highly. Measured by silhouette statistics, then your eye. Shape candidates are judged under path-traced light, so lighting errors cannot hide or fake a shape result.
2. **One default path.** Everything else stays in the code and is frozen: not tuned, not captured, not compared.
3. **One fixed scene card.** Five densities, four suns. Every round reports the same scorecard.
4. **Gates are numbers with bands, plus one owner look per phase.** "Mostly right" is an allowed result and is recorded as such.
5. **No LLM judges for taste.** Agents measure. You judge, on a few sheets per phase.
6. **Every place we use, change or drop a source's method goes in `DIVERGES.md`**: source, what it does, what we do, why, measured effect. It is a record of choices, not a list of debts. A divergence that looks or runs better is the intended result; one is "open" only when it is unmeasured or measurably worse.
7. **Small steps.** A sub-phase is one change, one scorecard, one sheet.
8. **Thresholds are fixed before a phase starts.** They are written into this file from what the previous phase measured, and are not moved during the phase without the owner's say. Numbers below marked "provisional" have not been through that yet.
9. **Small tasks, short runs.** Owner, 2026-11-21, tightened the same day after 90-minute and 60-minute agent tasks. One task is one change and one check: about 20 minutes of agent work, at most 5 minutes of rendering in total, a hand-back of a few lines and one sheet. No probes, ablations, multi-table reports or full-card runs unless a decision between two large options depends on them. Routine checks use the short card (the sky patch and EmberGen 02, at noon and backlit, 640x360). The full card runs once per phase, at its end. Anything longer needs a stated reason and the owner's yes. 500 MB per grid or buffer.
10. **Perception, not precision.** Owner, 2026-11-21. The reference renders exist to find errors a person can see, not to be matched to the percent. A difference under roughly 15 % in a region's brightness, or one nobody can spot in a side-by-side sheet, is not worth a task. Numbers are a guide to where to look; the gate is that the sheet reads the same at a glance, then the owner's eye. Chase precision only when it decides between two large options.

### Structure: a clean core, borrowing Guerrilla's layout where it serves

Revised 2026-11-21 after discussion with the owner.

Guerrilla runs three cloud systems side by side, fed by an authoring layer. That split (a sky-wide procedural system, voxel hero clouds, a generator and authoring on top) is a sound way to organise a cloud renderer, so we start from it. It is a starting layout, not a constraint: any part can be replaced by something that looks or runs better. The shaper and the lighting are rebuilt clean inside this repo, beside the old ones, which stay in the code frozen.

| System | Use | Our state | Plan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vertical profile (2.5D) | The sky at large | Have it, as one function mixing four generations (2015 recipe, 2017 map, 2022 tables, 2023 noise) | Rebuild small and clean. Start from the published structure (data fields, a vertical profile, noise carved from it; `docs/spec/vertical-profile-n3.md`) because it is cheap and proven at speed, then change whatever the photo measures and the owner's eye say is wrong. Much of the recipe was never published, so large parts are ours by necessity |
| Generator | Writes the data fields the profile system reads | Invented; theirs is unpublished | New, body-based: cloud bodies with a size distribution, a shared base level and their own top heights, written into the same five fields. One generator per layer |
| Voxel | Hero clouds, artist VDBs, fly-through | Have it; renders the pack and VDBs | Keep. It gets the new lighting model |
| Envelope | Cloud on terrain | Not built | Phase 5, if a demo needs it |
| Authoring | Painting, influence fields, weather states | Partly built | Keep; reconnect to the new fields in Phase 5 |

The old shaper, both old lighting models and the three styles remain selectable but are not tuned, captured or compared again.

### Phase 0: the truth rig

No look changes in this phase.

| Step | Work | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.1 Freeze | Tag. Name one baseline configuration. Mark other paths frozen. | Two runs of the card differ by less than the noise floor |
| 0.2 Path tracer | In the pathtracer repo (its own phase there, owner to schedule): an HG + Draine (or dual-lobe) phase, a max-depth switch usable for a single/multi split, a ground plane under a volume, AOVs allowed with volumes, and a high-g check | A g 0.85 slab and sphere match pbrt or Mitsuba within noise; the furnace test passes |
| 0.2b Cross-check | Render one card cloud in Karma (or Cycles) from the exported VDB and sky map | Karma and the pathtracer agree within noise. Until 0.2 lands, Karma is the reference and Phase 1 can start on it |
| 0.3 Linear compare | Read float radiance from both sides. Scorecard script: region luma ratios, shade B/R, band energy at three scales, fringe translucency | The scorecard reproduces PT1's findings; two path traces with different seeds give the noise floor |
| 0.4 The card | Parkouring, EmberGen 02 (your 8), the Disney cloud, one baked patch of the procedural sky, one thin stratus slab. Suns: noon, 20°, backlit, 5° | 20 references rendered and stored outside git, with a manifest |
| 0.5 Converged march | A runtime mode with 4x steps, no history and 4 samples per pixel | It is stable frame to frame and differs from the normal frame only where sampling artefacts are |

### Phase 1: light, fitted to the path trace

Build one new lighting model beside the old two. Fit its few constants to the references by script, not by hand.

**Gates, loosened 2026-11-21 by the owner (rule 10).** The noise floors Phase 0 measured are far below anything visible (reference ±0.2 %, realtime ±0.7 %), so the numbers below are perceptual guides, not precision targets. Each step is judged on the short card by a side-by-side sheet first and a rough number second.

| Step | Work | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 Sun shadow | Fix the large errors in the sun's optical depth that step 1.1 found (the 8-bit density floor, the 1.2 scale). Smaller ones are left alone | Sun-only single scatter within about 15 % by region on the short card. Done when the sky patch no longer shows a false deep shadow |
| 1.2 Multiple scatter | One fitted term for the light that has scattered more than once. No surface-scale occlusion on it | Bodies within about 15 % of the reference at noon and backlit, and lit faces no visibly rougher than the reference |
| 1.3 Sky and ground light | Ambient from above and the ground bounce | Shade and bases read the same colour and weight as the reference on the sheet |
| 1.4 Thin material | Fringes and wisps | Fringes read translucent and sky-tinted like the reference on the sheet |
| 1.5 Owner look | One sheet per cloud: path trace, new, old | You say "mostly right" or name what is off |

Steps 1.3 and 1.4 are done only if the 1.2 sheet shows they are still visibly off. The aim is to reach the 1.5 sheet quickly and let the owner's eye direct what is left.

Where today's two models stand against these gates (`work/captures/card/scorecard.md`): thick bodies range from 0.24x to 1.12x of the reference, lit-face fine texture from 1.0x to 4.6x, fringe B/R is 0.2 to 1.0 below the reference at noon and low sun. Neither model passes any step on any cloud.

A Horizon-like look, if wanted later, is an optional style on top of the fitted model (a floret-contrast dial). It is not a target of this phase.

### Phase 2: shape

This is the phase most likely to change how the sky looks. It starts with a check that costs little.

| Step | Work | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2.0 Confirm | Bake a patch of today's procedural sky and path trace it | A sheet. If it still reads as slabs with fuzz, shape is confirmed as the main defect. If it looks good, stop and return to Phase 1 |
| 2.1 Measure | From alpha masks of your photos, the liked VDBs and our renders: height-to-width of bodies, lobe size spectrum of the silhouette, base flatness, gap sizes | The numbers separate photos from today's sky clearly. If they don't, fix the measure first |
| 2.2 Clean shaper | A small shaper written new, starting from the published profile structure, fed first by a hand-made test field. Free to depart from the source wherever that looks or runs better | On the same field it draws clouds no worse than the old shaper by the 2.1 numbers, in a fraction of the code and settings |
| 2.3 Generator: bodies | Bodies with a size distribution and their own top heights, from humilis to congestus in one sky, written into the five fields | Height-to-width distribution and gap sizes inside the photo range |
| 2.4 Generator: lobes | Each body built from nested lobes, so the fields carry mid-scale structure before any erosion | Lobe spectrum has energy at the middle scales, within the photo range |
| 2.5 Erosion discipline | Erosion works on edges and undersides only and never below the pixel footprint | Lit-face fine-band energy stays inside the Phase 1 band with the new shapes |
| 2.6 Bases | Soft, flat, darker to the centre | Base flatness in range; no contour lines in the converged march |
| 2.7 Hero VDBs | Your simulated clouds placed in the procedural sky as the showcase path | One frame you would publish |

Each step ends with one sheet for you: photo, ours under path-traced light, ours realtime.

### Phase 3: image

Only now look at hatch, sparkle, softness and smear, against the converged march. Thresholds are set at the end of Phase 2 from the converged march's own noise floor.

| Step | Work | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 Still frame | Step size, jitter and surface handling at Cinematic | Error against the converged march under the threshold on every card frame; fixed-pattern (hatch) energy at the noise floor |
| 3.2 Motion | Temporal blend and history under camera and wind motion | A motion clip passes the frame-to-frame stability measure; a moving frame is no softer than a still one beyond the threshold |
| 3.3 Levels | Desktop and Laptop through the upscaler | Each level passes 3.1 and 3.2 with its own, looser thresholds |
| 3.4 Owner look | One still sheet and one clip per level | You say "mostly right" or name what is off |

### Phase 4: speed

Target 4 ms desktop and 3 ms laptop, measured on a quiet machine. The allowed scorecard drop per step is set at the end of Phase 3. Each row is one step and passes on both columns.

| Step | Work | Gate: cost | Gate: look |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4.0 Baseline | A clean timing breakdown of the new core on a quiet machine | A per-pass table that two runs reproduce within 10 % | none |
| 4.1 Low-resolution trace | The trace at the Desktop and Laptop scales through the upscaler | provisional: 12 ms desktop | Phase 3 thresholds for that level |
| 4.2 Cached lighting | Sun and sky light from a shadow map or light volume, not per-sample cone taps | provisional: 8 ms desktop | Phase 1 bands still pass |
| 4.3 March cost | Step counts, empty-space skipping, horizon band | provisional: 6 ms desktop | Phase 3 thresholds |
| 4.4 Cadence | Lighting and far field updated over several frames | 4 ms desktop, 3 ms laptop | Phase 3 motion gate |

A step that misses its cost gate but keeps the look is a partial pass and is recorded as such.

### Phase 5: breadth

Cirrus, mid-level layers, storms, weather states and painting resume only after Phases 1 to 3 pass. Each gets one card scene and the same three truths.

### Phase 6: package and release

API, bundle and docs for the one default path. Frozen paths ship only if they pass the same card.

### How agents run it

- One orchestrator per phase. Sub-phases go to agents with a fixed brief: the step, the gate, the card, the scorecard command.
- Agents hand back a scorecard row, one sheet and any new `DIVERGES.md` entries. Nothing else.
- A failed gate is a result. The agent reports what it tried and stops. It does not widen the change.
- You see one sheet per sub-phase at most, and one decision per phase.

## What I did not verify

- I did not run the bench. Findings on the image come from stored captures dated 2026-11-19 and 20.
- The Takram comparison is my read of its design, not a side-by-side.
- That the sky's slab shape survives correct lighting is a prediction. Step 2.0 tests it.
- All stored timings were taken on a loaded machine.
