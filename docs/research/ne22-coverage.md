# Nubis Evolved (2022) coverage: what we have, what is missing, what to do next

Audit date 2026-10-13. Owner goal: reach the Nubis Evolved 2022 (NE22) level of procedural clouds for everything that looks good without blowing the performance budget. Nubis Cubed (2023) continues the line with artist-made voxel clouds and is out of scope here, except where noted.

Read-only audit of: `sources/ne22.md` (page index, listings), `hzd-nubis-evolved.md`, `topics/*.md`, `nubis-lod.md`, `sources/n3.md` s1-46, the reports `work/reports/{H1,H5,H6,H7,W1,F1,F2}.md`, the board (`work/BOARD.md`: F1, F2, W1, N1, L3, LOD1, W2, L2), and the code under `packages/runtime/src/` (`procedural/`, `procedural/hzd/`, `sky-layer.ts`, `shaders/sky-layer.wgsl.ts`, `cirrus/`, `generate/`). Page numbers are NE22 PDF pages (= slide numbers). Nothing is quoted from the deck beyond short phrases; costs marked "est." are my estimates, not measurements.

## Reading the status column

- Ours means the `hzd` shaper in `procedural/` (the HZD/Nubis procedural sky, the default procedural path) unless a row says otherwise. The G path (`generate/` + `sky-layer.ts`, the NVDF sky layer) and the voxel march are the Nubis Cubed side and are only mentioned when they cover a gap.
- Costs are GPU ms for the sky layer at ultra, 1920x1080, on the dev machine. The F1/F2 reports measured the sky layer at roughly 13-22 ms (noisy, shared GPU); H6 vs the current default differs by +1.6 to +3.4 ms. NE22's own budget is far below that (see the performance row).

## Temporal reprojection, ghosting, and the upscaler

The NE22 deck says little in words about the skies' temporal system; it inherits Guerrilla's 16-frame reconstruction from HZD (a 4x4 block, one pixel traced per frame, the rest reprojected from the previous frame). The two places where NE22 shows reprojection code are the superstorm (p.155) and the sky clouds (p.157). The fix for ghosting there is not a cleverer history filter. It is a correct motion vector: the pixel's cloud world position, from the stored hit distance, is moved by the same rotation the density uses (and, for the sky, by the 2D scroll), blended by the storm mask, and then projected into the previous frame. The Forbidden West talk (`sources/hfw.md`, p.63-70) says this is what allowed a realistic rotation speed instead of "pixel soup", and that it let sky clouds scroll much faster. The deck shows no neighbourhood clamp or ghost rejection of its own.

Ours already has the 4x4 Bayer sampling, a reprojection through the cloud's opacity-weighted mean distance, variance clipping at 1.5 sigma to reject a history that no longer matches, and a sub-pixel jitter that changes per 16-frame cycle (`SKY_RESOLVE_WGSL` in `shaders/sky-layer.wgsl.ts`). What it lacks is exactly NE22's piece: the reprojection uses camera matrices only, so wind-driven and rotating clouds smear (W1 saw it in the fast-wind clips, `hfw.md` notes the same gap). Adding the cloud-motion term is task 5, and it is a prerequisite for storms.

For the owner's FSR3-class upscaler (`/Users/dex/Developer/upscaler`, `docs/research/upscaler-integration.md`): it expects colour, depth, a velocity texture, and an optional or auto-generated reactive mask, and it owns camera jitter. That matches the integration doc's option B (reconstruct the cloud layer before composition) and the NE22 lesson: the cloud layer needs its own motion vectors, because scene velocity does not describe wind or rotation. A plan that fits both: trace the cloud layer at a lower resolution with one jittered sample per pixel instead of one per 4x4 block, supply NE-style cloud motion vectors computed at the opacity-weighted mean distance, keep transmittance in the alpha (the doc warns this packing must be validated), and mark fast-changing regions (storms, state transitions, lightning) reactive. Then the 1/16 Bayer resolve becomes the fallback path, not a second accumulator on top of the upscaler's own history, and a single object owns the jitter. Two temporal histories stacked would add ghosting, which is the failure the doc's section C warns about, so only one should be on at a time.
