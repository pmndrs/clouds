## In one paragraph

This range has no new maths and no code. Its unique content is (1) the high frame rate budget: the near/far split
resolution trick [s157–160]; (2) **how Guerrilla authored voxel cloudscapes**: one hero formation at a time, composed for
the sun arc, built from several rotated sub-volumes, tied in with a flat wispy skirt, carved with tunnels, then
"frankenclouded" into a coastline cloudscape and re-used for cinematics by translating the whole grid [s161–171];
(3) the **only dynamic voxel cloud in the game**, the Stormbird storm: an offline fluid-sim plume, scaled up and
frankenclouded, lit from inside by superstorm lightning tech, with a scripted dissipation whose method the talk does not
explain [s175–189]; (4) the closing capability table, which says outright that voxel clouds have **pseudomotion only**,
and that real evolution needs realtime voxel evolution plus on-the-fly SDF generation [s173, s190].

## Slide by slide

### Voxel Clouds / Rendering: the high frame rate budget [s156–160]
- **s156** (title; night teal storm still, image181). The PS5 modes are 30, 40 and 60 Hz. At 30 Hz voxel clouds "just
  squeak by"; 60 Hz gives half the frame time.
- **s157** (diagram). This is the split already used for the Envelope method, reused unchanged for voxel clouds: the march
  is split at **200 m** along the ray, with the near part at **480×270** and the far part at **960×540**.
  - Reasoning: far needs the higher resolution to stop aliasing. Near is where the march is most expensive, and blur there
    is hidden by low density and motion.
  - The notes recall that temporal upscaling was dropped for these methods.
  - The diagram's axis labels are swapped (960 is drawn as the height); use the text slide s160 for the numbers.
- **s158** (table, two stills image182/183). One frame: **30 Hz @ 960×540 = 4 ms**; **40/60 Hz @ 960×540 + 480×270 =
  2.1 ms**.
  - **The split is the high frame rate path.** 30 Hz mode renders the whole ray at 960×540 in one pass.
  - The near/far resolution ratio is exactly 1:2 per axis (1:4 pixels).
- **s159** (video, 30 vs 60 Hz side by side). Cost of the split: a bit more pixelation on **sharp, dense** features in the
  60 Hz run. Low-density regions hide it. The flight experience is unchanged.
- **s160** (summary). Two passes, <200 m at 480×270, >200 m at 960×540. Saves about 50%. 40 Hz uses the same method.

### Voxel Clouds / Production: how the cloudscape was authored [s161–171]
- **s161**. A dev-build still: the words "In Progress… :-)" written in voxel clouds over the coast (image185). It shows
  freeform modelling: anything can be a cloud.
- **s162** "Chickenscratch layout" (pencil sketch, image186; the best composition figure in the deck). Before modelling,
  Schneider sketched the sky as seen along the **east–west and north–south axes**, because those are the views against
  which silhouettes must compose with the **arc of the sun**.
  - The sketch shows the east view (sun low, centre) with towering cumulus framing the sun and banks to either side.
  - The south view shows separated cumulus heaps of different heights, with flat bases near one level, and a low
    stratiform band.
- **s163** (video media24, 36 s). The order of work, from the notes:
  1. Place **one large cloud** so it looks good **from the ground, from several angles, at every time of day**.
  2. Add other features and **connective tissue** to tie it into the rest of the cloudscape.
  3. For some clouds, add **tunnels and caves** with the Atlas tools.
- *(video)* What the frames add to the notes:
  - The cloud is placed in the Decima editor and is made of **about 4–5 overlapping, individually rotated boxes**:
    sub-volumes at different yaw and tilt, moved with a transform gizmo.
  - It is checked under sunset, day and dusk light from the same ground view.
  - Then a **flat, wispy, horizontally spread skirt** is added around the base, on a black debug background. It
    is thin, ragged and wider than the body, and is the "connective tissue" that links the cloud to the layer.
  - The tunnel is authored as a **polygon mesh path** (shown magenta inside the cloud's mesh) used as a cutout. Then
    there is a fly-through.
- **s164–165** (videos). The team also checked every cloud from the air.
  - Inside tunnels "the lighting just works". The light quality changes inside and toward the back of the tunnel; this is
    the voxel light grid, see earlier slides.
  - **Wing-tip streamers**: particles are emitted **whenever intersection with the cloud is detected**. Gameplay therefore
    has a cloud-occupancy query at arbitrary points. The talk does not say how (SDF or density lookup, CPU or GPU).
- **s166**. A phone photo of a real cumulus with a tunnel-like hole, to justify the caves.
- **s167** (video media27, Atlas/Houdini). "We did this multiple times for each cloud formation and combined them all into
  a cloudscape."
  - *(video)* Over a terrain height map, the formations are added one by one. The result is a **long continuous chain
    hugging the coastline**, a field of smaller separate heaps inland, and one isolated flat cloud offshore. The variety
    comes from cluster size and spacing, not from one repeated shape.
- **s168**. Four gameplay views (images 192–195), mainly ground level.
  - Puffy cumulus with cirrus streaks above. A red sunset beach with dark banks.
- **s169** (video media28; still image196). For cinematics the **whole cloudscape is repositioned per shot by a
  transform**, which gives variation with no extra memory.
  - The still is an editor panel: a `SetVoxelCloudTransformEventResource` with an Event group (Enabled, Locked, Start
    Frame, Stop Frame) and a General group with **Translation only** (x, y, z).
  - At the capture's resolution the values read about (3134, −2847, 544), with Stop Frame ≈ 205. These are uncertain.
    The undo history shows Y being tried at several values (153, −2747, −4847 …).
  - So a shot event translates the grid for a frame range. No rotation or scale field is visible.
- **s170** (images 197/198, video media29). The **boss arena**: a cloudscape built in Houdini that wraps around the play
  area.
  - In Houdini it is a ring of congestus towers around an open centre.
  - In game it reads as a grey storm wall behind the fight.
- **s171** (summary).
  - "Frankenclouding works."
  - The workflow is a paradigm shift: you can model anything, even text.
  - Cinematics re-use the cloudscape through transforms.
  - Bespoke cloudscapes, for boss fights and so on, are easy.

### The Stormbird Encounter: the one evolving voxel cloud [s175–189]
- **s175**: design brief.
  - The Stormbird is an apex predator that locally makes small storm clouds with lightning. It could not be shown in
    earlier games because the tech did not exist.
  - Encounter (Elijah Houck): a cat-and-mouse fight inside its lair, a storm cloud. Sneak attacks, then it kills your
    mount and you glide down.
- **s176** (video media31, 112 s). The cloud **dissipates after the Stormbird dies**.
  - *(video)* The fight is inside a dense teal-lit interior. The last ~20 s (t ≈ 88–108 s) is looking up from the
    beach, in rain, after "several minutes later…" title cards.
  - Over **about 15–20 s** the solid overhead mass **breaks into separated patches, then into streaky, stretched wisps**,
    and stars show through. The remnants hold their place: no visible drift, no advection.
  - This *looks like* a density, coverage or threshold ramp under which the erosion eats the low-profile regions first and
    leaves wispy remnants. **The talk does not describe the mechanism** (inference only).
  - The rain fades with it.
- **s177**: the tasks.
  1. Exterior with lightning.
  2. An interior that is "not just a cave".
  3. Dissipate on the Stormbird's death.
- **s178** (image203, an HFW superstorm still with red lightning). The aim is a **highly localized** thunderstorm, not a
  Forbidden West superstorm.
- **s179** (video media32, 4 s). **First attempt: a fluid sim** of a standing plume with a skirt of wispy material at the
  bottom.
  - *(video)* A small low lump grows over 4 s into a taller plume with turrets. Ragged wisps trail off the lower side,
    forming the skirt.
- **s180** (image205). That sim in game, with **inner glow from the HFW superstorm glow code**.
  - The plume is small and flat-bottomed and hangs clear of the ground.
  - Lesson 1: far too small to hold an interior large enough for the encounter.
- **s181** (video media33, turntable). "I scaled it up and **frankenclouded** the heck out of it": this is the final model.
  - *(video)* A broad mass about 1.5× wider than tall. Ragged horizontal tongues stick out at the base, the body is
    cauliflower, and one taller turret leans to the side.
  - This is the `StormbirdCloud` NVDF in the pack, about 67×100×54 voxels per nubis-sources.
- **s182** (image205 again). Lesson 2: the glow implied an **opening on the underside**, so internal lighting could be
  used to reveal openings.
- **s183** (video). Lightning drives the reveal:
  - Every **ground discharge** lights the interior and shows its structure.
  - Extra internal flashes suggest a build-up.
- **s184** (video). The HFW **local weather system** makes a localized rainstorm under the cloud.
- **s185**: the exterior from farther away (image165, video media20). The interior goal is "weird and terrifying but
  functional" for a human, cosy for a Stormbird.
- **s186** (images 209/210). The **"Egg"** is the focal point that keeps the unnatural storm alive.
  - Tesla-style arcs build charge until a ground discharge fires through a hole under the egg.
  - Killing the bird dissipates both the egg and the cloud.
  - Implementation: superstorm lighting tech **adapted for voxel clouds**. The details are in the HFW talk, not here.
- **s187** (image211, Atlas/Houdini). The cave and the egg are **cut out with the Atlas tools**, with plenty of pockets for
  ambushes.
  - The image is a voxel-block view. The egg is an ellipsoid of a few dozen voxels in a hollowed chamber; the shape is
    coarse at NVDF resolution, and the up-rez noise supplies all the detail.
- **s188** (video). There are **enough openings for light to enter**, so the space looks different at every time of day.
- **s189** (summary). Credits easy 3D modelling, existing lightning tech, the existing local weather system, and work
  with quest design and audio.
  - Clouds were treated as physical things you can touch, build with and **link to game scripts**.

That is needed to reproduce s176 cheaply |

**Gaps that matter for the hero target** (a cumulus line where a large body overtakes and swallows smaller ones while
ejecting volume upward):
- The deck gives **no runtime merging, advection or sim**. Voxel clouds are static plus pseudomotion. The only animated
  shapes are offline fluid sims frozen into one frame (s179) and a scripted fade (s176).
- The relevant lessons are about authoring:
  - Grow the shape offline with a sim.
  - Scale it up and composite it.
  - Treat dynamic behaviour as script-driven, swapped or faded volumes.
- The capability the deck names as future work is realtime voxel evolution plus on-the-fly SDF regeneration. We already
  have the SDF half (`stamp.ts:489`, `evolution.ts`). What is missing is a way to drive authored volumes over time, for
  example:
  - baked Pyro keyframes of a merge, played through `swapVolume` (`runtime.ts:251`);
  - or GPU blending or advection of two stamped NVDFs, followed by an SDF rebuild.

## Contradictions with existing notes
1. **`nubis-sources.md` §6** presents the two-pass split as the voxel method in general. s158 shows that 30 Hz mode
   renders one 960×540 pass (4 ms); the split is the 40/60 Hz path.
2. **`deck-scenes.md`** lists "s170, s172 … second still in monochrome" as the boss arena. The s172 still (image199) is
   the Conclusions title image, not the arena. It also describes s163 as "wireframe boxes then final bright cumulus". The
   video shows several rotated sub-volume boxes, a skirt added afterwards, and a mesh tunnel cutout; that note should
   gain these details.
3. Not a contradiction but worth noting: the s157 diagram swaps its width and height labels. The text slide s160 is
   authoritative.
