## Section map
- **s110–115 Voxel light grid.** The sun light march leaves the view ray: 2 in-line samples, then a precomputed 256×256×32 summed-density grid. −40 % render time, long inter-cloud shadows.
- **s116–125 Multiple scattering.** The dimensional profile is used as an in-scatter probability field, with Beer–Lambert relaxed toward the sun and deep inside. Gives inner glow and dark edges.
- **s126–134 Ambient and secondary light.** Profile-based ambient × exp(−summed density toward the sky) from the same grid; lightning glow; TOD and summary.
- **s135–146 SDF for the march.** Sphere tracing. The SDF NVDF (512×512×64, −256…4096 m) is BC1-compressed with a custom round-down 565 scalar encoding (bonus s144–145), and the memory per method is compared.
- **s147–155 The march itself.** Ray bounds, step = max(SDF, adaptive √t·0.08 ≥ 1 m), jitter animated < 250 m, sampling only where SDF < 0, full HLSL (s150), PS5 cost tables.

## Slide-by-slide

### Lighting: voxel light grid (s110–115, s134)
- **s110–111.** Light energy = Direct + Ambient + Secondary (lightning). The direct term's secondary march toward the light is the cost the team has fought for years [s111].
- **s112.** Decoupling the light march from the view ray was an old idea of Nathan Vos. It only became worth the complexity once the data were voxels.
- **s113.** Of the light-ray samples, **the first two are taken in-line as before**; the rest come from a pass that precomputes them into a **256×256×32** voxel grid, which is read after the first two samples. The notes say this "preserves detail near the surfaces" and is more diffuse beyond. The precompute runs outside the ray-march. The distances of the two in-line samples are not given in this range. Figure: p.129 (camera ray, red in-line light samples, green grid lookup).
- **s114.** Build: for each voxel **that contains cloud**, march to the grid boundary toward the sun and accumulate density per step. Results:
  - **~40 % lower render time.**
  - The build costs **0.1–0.2 ms**, depending on time of day and ray length, **amortised over 8 frames**.
  - Figure: p.130 (grid box 256×256×32 with rays to the sun).
- **s115.** First long inter-cloud shadows. Comparison: the old **256 m, 10-sample** light march against the voxel lighting (images image140/141, p.131–132). "An optimization produced a much better result."
- **s134 (summary).** Summed density is precomputed and stored for "some samples". Amortised 0.1–0.2 ms every 8 frames, −40 %, long shadows. The same grid also improved ambient, and many approximations got simpler by just looking up the dimensional profile.

### Lighting: multiple scattering from the dimensional profile (s116–125)
- **s116–119 (reasoning).** Uses photos of crystal growths and clouds:
  - Near a cloud's surface there is little material, so most light keeps going and does not reach the eye. That gives the **dark edges**.
  - Deeper in, scattering becomes more isotropic, so the chance of in-scatter toward the viewer rises with depth. A volume of light "shines through" the surface (a flashlight behind cotton). That gives the **inner glow**.
  - Model it geometrically as a scattering-potential field, not physically.
- **s120.** That field already exists: it is the **dimensional profile** (renders image145/146, p.137–138; the profile looks like a soft blurred version of the cloud).
- **s121–122.** Slice demo: the cloud aligned between viewer and sun, sliced, all light energies off (p.139–141).
- **s123, the formula** (code `n3-s123.hlsl`):
  ```
  ms_volume  = dimensional_profile;
  ms_volume *= exp(-inSunLightSummedDensitySamples
                   * Remap(sun_dot, 0.0, 0.9, 0.25, ValueRemap(cloud_distance, -128.0, 0.0, 0.05, 0.25)));
  ```
  - `cloud_distance` is the SDF at the sample (metres, negative inside).
  - The extinction multiplier is **0.25** sideways and at the surface. It falls to **0.05** only when looking toward the sun (sun_dot → 0.9) **and** ≥128 m deep.
  - The notes: Beer–Lambert because scattered light is eventually absorbed; the curve is "relaxed for vectors near the sun and near the inside of the cloud".
  - **Ambiguity:**
    - `ValueRemap` is clamped (Nubis pack `VoxelCloudSampler.cg:4–10`).
    - Plain `Remap` is not defined in the deck. If it is unclamped like GPU Pro 7's Remap, then for sun_dot < 0 (sun behind the viewer) the multiplier exceeds 0.25, up to 0.47 at sun_dot = −1 with X = 0.05. That is more absorption, so less glow, when front-lit.
    - The deck never says what `sun_dot` is. Probably dot(view dir, sun dir).
    - Units and scale of `inSunLightSummedDensitySamples` are not given.
  - Figure p.142 shows the ms field alone on the slice: a bright core and a dim rim.
- **s124–125.** Shipped renders: sun in front without and with ms (image155/156, p.143–144), and sun behind without and with (image157/158, p.145–146). With ms, the backlit body fills with warm glow while edges stay darker, so **lobes separate by brightness**. This is the strongest "lighting reveals billows" evidence in the range.

### Lighting: ambient and secondary (s126–133)
- **s126.** `ambient_scattering = pow(1.0 - dimensional_profile, 0.5)`. This is the same as the 2.5D clouds (s30): the inverse profile is the probability that sky light reaches the point and scatters to the eye. Renders without and with ambient: s127 (image159/160, p.148–149).
- **s128.** Directional ambient, from the same grid with a summed density **toward the sky** (code `n3-s128.hlsl`):
  ```
  ambient_scattering = pow(1.0 - dimensional_profile, 0.5) * exp(-summed_ambient_density);
  ```
  The ambient gets weaker where clouds above occlude the sky. There is no floor and no ground term in the formula. Figure s129 (image161/162, p.151–152): with directionality, undersides and the lower body darken and the cauliflower top stays bright. That vertical contrast is what reads as **layered, 3-D form**.
- **s130.** Lightning is also a probability field (code `n3-s130.hlsl`):
  ```
  potential_energy   = pow(1.0 - d1 / radius, 12.0);       // spherical falloff from the flash
  pseudo_attenuation = 1.0 - saturate(density * 5.0);      // fakes non-homogeneous density
  glow_energy        = potential_energy * pseudo_attenuation;
  ```
  Diagram labels: sample position, light pos, d1, radius (p.153).
- **s131–133.** Videos only (media20–22, not extracted): lightning preview, a TOD cycle check, long-distance shadows ("make quite a difference").

### Ray-marching: why an SDF (s135–139)
- **s136.** Without knowing where clouds are relative to a sample, successive samples can't be placed efficiently.
- **s137.** The envelope clouds used cone step mapping plus distance step mapping over 2D authoring data (Dummer 2006). The voxel problem is fully 3-D.
- **s138.** Sphere tracing (Hart 1995). The SDF gives the largest safe step, positive outside and negative inside.
- **s139.** References: Quilez's SDF articles, and Aaltonen's Claybook slides for using SDFs in high-performance cases.

### SDF data and compression (s140–146)
- **s140.** When the "frankencloudscape" (composited cloudscape) is finished, a 3-D SDF is generated as a separate NVDF:
  - **512×512×64**, the same grid as the modeling NVDF.
  - Range **−256 m to 4096 m, rescaled to 0–1**.
  - Figure p.163: top-view cloudscape → SDF (brighter with distance) → field box.
- **s141.** It is kept separate from the modeling NVDFs (profile, detail type, density scale; BC6, 1 B/texel, 16.777 MB) because it is read **every step**. Bundling the two would create a memory bottleneck.
- **s142.** Nathan Reed's BCn table. BC4 (one channel) looks ideal, but precision loss is asymmetric:
  - a decoded value that is **too low** costs extra steps;
  - **too high** gives rendering errors (stepping past the surface).
- **s143.** The fix is a custom BC1 compressor (Hugh Malan) giving **close to 16-bit precision at 0.5 B/texel**, against 2 B/texel uncompressed. It works because SDF data is smooth. It saves **10–30 %** depending on ray length.
  - Decode: `dot(sampled_color.rgb, float3(1.0, 0.03529415, 0.00069204))`.
  - Figure image173 (p.166): uncompressed vs BC1 SDF slices look identical.
- **s144 (bonus).**
  - Each DXT1 4×4 block holds two 16-bit endpoints and 2 bits per texel.
  - The encoded value must be **≤ the true value**, or sphere tracing can step inside or past the isosurface.
  - Idea: 16-bit value as 565, with R = top 5 bits, G = next 6, B = low 5. Bilinear sample, then `dot(rgb, (1, 1/32, 1/(32*64)))`.
  - Bilinear filtering stays correct across a carry: the notes' worked example (29 | 30 → 29.5) shows it.
  - Figure: DXT1 texture (false colour) vs decoded result (p.167).
- **s145 (bonus).** Real 565 endpoints don't decode linearly, because the GPU expands them with odd rounding (refs: Castaño 2009, Giesen 2021). So G is scaled up to cover the **largest possible R step**, and B likewise for G.
  - Encoder (code `n3-s145.hlsl`): take the largest R level ≤ the value, then the largest G ≤ the residual, then B.
  - Each of the 16 texels picks the closest of the 4 palette values that is still ≤ the true value.
  - The high endpoint is pulled down from the block max to improve average error.
  - *Our check of the shipped constants (inference, consistent with the notes):* 5-bit expansion `(r<<3)|(r>>2)` steps by 8 or 9 /255, and 6-bit by 4 or 5 /255. So the largest R step is 9/255 = **0.0352941** (= the G weight), and the B weight is (9/255)·(5/255) = 45/65025 = **0.00069204**. Both shipped constants match. Decoded range ≈ [0, 1.036].
- **s146. Memory per cloudscape:** vertical profile 0.541 MB, envelope 9.437 MB, voxel **25.166 MB**. Our check: 16.777 (modeling, BC6) + 8.389 (SDF, BC1: 512·512·64·0.5 B) = 25.166 ✓.
  - Precision context (our arithmetic): at 16 bits the 4352 m range gives ~0.066 m/LSB; at 8 bits it would be 17 m/LSB.

### The march (s147–150)
- **s147. Ray bounds.** The ray starts at the camera and ends at the **nearer of the voxel-grid box exit and the geometry depth**.
- **s148. Step = SDF + adaptive + jitter.**
  - The SDF places steps outside clouds.
  - The adaptive step grows with distance and places steps inside clouds, so distant clouds take larger steps while the gaps between clouds are still skipped optimally.
  - `max(sdf, adaptive)` switches to the adaptive step automatically once the SDF goes negative.
  - Jitter is **animated near the camera and static far away**: it counters undersampling without making distant clouds shimmer.
  - Figure p.172: SDF steps (hollow) then adaptive steps (filled).
  - *The pseudocode adds jitter to the step; the shipped HLSL (s150) does not*; see the code file header.
- **s149.** Sample only if `sdf_cloud_distance < 0.0`. A sample means density, direct, ambient and secondary light energy, then integration along the view ray.
- **s150 (bonus, shipped HLSL, `n3-s150.hlsl`).** Exact constants:
  - The loop runs while `T > view_ray_transmittance_limit` (value not given) and `t < min(segment.end, mMaxDistance)`.
  - `adaptive = max(1.0, max(sqrt(t), EPSILON) * 0.08)`. That is **1 m up to t = 156 m**, 1.79 m at 500 m, 2.53 m at 1 km, 4 m at 2.5 km, 8 m at 10 km.
  - `step = max(sdf, adaptive)`.
  - `jitter = (t < 250 ? mJitteredHash : mStaticHash) * step`. **It moves only the sample position**; the advance is `t += step`.
  - The SDF is fetched every step.
  - The sample position is rebuilt from the camera each step, "to save VGPRs".

### Performance (s151–155)
- **s151.** Cost should scale with what is on screen; clouds and geometry should trade budget. All numbers are at 960×540.
- **s152–154.** Cloud ms on PS5 (geometry ms in brackets):

  | View | Base | + voxel lighting | + adaptive & SDF march |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Ground (s152) | 10 | 6.1 | **2.2** (geo 7) |
  | Between clouds and land (s153) | 12 | 8.2 | **4.0** (geo 5) |
  | Inside clouds (s154) | 10 | 8.0 | **4.0** (geo 4) |

  s153: the 12 ms comes from rays grazing cloud undersides. **Offsetting cloud heights to shorten those rays** mitigates it.
- **s155 (summary).** Compressed SDF, a hybrid of SDF, adaptive and jittered steps, 2.2–4 ms, and performance that scales.

## Deck vs our runtime (voxel/NVDF path)
Runtime files: `packages/runtime/src/shaders/march.wgsl.ts`, `lighting.wgsl.ts`, `light-grid.wgsl.ts`, `volume.wgsl.ts`, `light-grid.ts`, `resources.ts`, `runtime.ts`.

**Same as the deck**
- Sampling is gated on `sdf < 0` (`march.wgsl.ts:211`).
- `step = max(sdf, adaptive)` (`:202`).
- Jitter scaled by step that moves the sample, not the advance (`:215`).
- Ray from the box entry to the min of box exit, scene depth and cap (`:136–140`).
- SDF never overstates the distance: r16float **rounded down** (`resources.ts:29–31`), minus half a voxel for trilinear (`volume.wgsl.ts:68`). That is the deck's round-down principle, at 2 B/texel uncompressed, the deck's own baseline.
- Light grid dims `ceil(volume/2)` (`light-grid.ts:28–30`), which gives the deck's **256×256×32** for a 512×512×64 NVDF.
- Ambient uses the grid's up-sum (`light-grid.wgsl.ts:105`, `lighting.wgsl.ts:61`).
- The lightning wisp term is verbatim (`march.wgsl.ts:64`).

**Mismatches** (shape and detail impact first)
1. **Step size inside clouds is 2.5–3× coarser.**
   - Deck: `max(1, √t·0.08)`. Ours: `max(3, √t·0.2)·stepScale` (`march.wgsl.ts:110–111, 202`).
   - At 500 m: 1.8 m vs 4.5 m. At 2 km: 3.6 m vs 8.9 m. stepScale is 1.5–2 on medium and low (`runtime.ts:72–74`).
   - Ours also widens steps in thin or occluded media (P3-E, `:207–208`) and after 256 steps (`:210`).
   - Impact: the deck's 1 m floor out to 156 m is what lets the 50–150 m HF fold and the eroded billows (s109) resolve. Coarse steps blur surface detail into a soft mass. **Most likely shape/detail cost in this range.**
2. **Ambient has floors the deck doesn't.**
   - Deck: `sqrt(1−P)·exp(−S_up)`, which can go fully dark under a thick top.
   - Ours: `mix(ambientFloor, 1, sqrt(1−P)) · mix(down, up·mix(ambientFloor, 1, exp(−0.08·S_up·k)), heightBias)` (`lighting.wgsl.ts:61–67`).
   - Defaults (`runtime.ts:424–425`): `ambientFloor` 0.15, `ambientExtinction` 0.08. Ours also adds a ground term and AMBIENT_GAIN 1.5.
   - Impact: flatter bases, so less vertical contrast between layers (compare s129).
3. **The ms relaxation is not view-dependent by default.**
   - Ours: `relax = mix(0.25, valueRemap(sdf,−128,0,0.05,0.25), max(sunRelax, glowFront))` with `glowFront = 1` default (`lighting.wgsl.ts:58`, `runtime.ts:425`). So the deep-interior relaxation applies at every view angle; the deck applies it only toward the sun.
   - Ours also multiplies ms by `1 + glowBoost·sunRelax` (glowBoost 12) and a billow micro-occlusion (`:59`).
   - Possible extra deck behaviour if `Remap` is unclamped: stronger absorption when front-lit.
   - Impact: front-lit clouds glow from inside, so the dark-edge/bright-core contrast that outlines lobes (s124–125) weakens. The runtime compensates with `cloudEdgeFactor` (`lighting.wgsl.ts:136–138`), which is not in the deck.
4. **In-line light samples.**
   - Deck: "first two as before", then the grid.
   - Ours: a micro sample (4 m) + two in-line samples (0–16 m, 16–48 m) + the grid at +48 m (`march.wgsl.ts:226–243`; `lighting.wgsl.ts:8–9`). The in-line samples are reused within 4 m (`:115, 227`).
   - Not a contradiction: the deck doesn't give its distances (check NE 2022 / s28 range).
5. **Grid build differs.**
   - Deck: march only occupied voxels to the box boundary; 8-frame amortisation.
   - Ours: all texels march a 2×-reduced volume with a geometric step (`light-grid.wgsl.ts:52–65, 87–107`), plus the sun path beyond the box and through procedural clouds (P9-C3, X4). The rebuild spreads over **16 frames** at 256×256×32 (`light-grid.ts:15–18`).
   - Ours does not skip empty texels in the in-box march; the deck does ("if a voxel has cloud in it").
6. **Jitter is animated at all distances in ours** (`march.wgsl.ts:212–215`); the deck uses a static hash past 250 m. Ours is a deliberate change: IGN printed a fixed hatch. The deck pairs its static far hash with no temporal upscaler.
7. **SDF fetches.** The deck fetches every step, so compression matters. Ours skips fetches with a Lipschitz bound inside clouds (`march.wgsl.ts:185–201`); this is not in the deck. Not using BC1 is perf only.
8. **Extras not in the deck:**
   - deep-scatter octaves (`lighting.wgsl.ts:68–75`);
   - environment hand-off at low sun (`:76–83`);
   - an early-out residual renormalisation (`march.wgsl.ts:264–270`);
   - a lightning core term with occlusion (`:65`, documented deviation).
9. **HF detail switch.** Ours uses `ts < frame.misc.x` (detailDistance 150 m on high, `march.wgsl.ts:218`). The deck shows no switch in this range; s109's comment says 150 m.
