## In one paragraph
The deck is in two halves. Slides 1–46 recap the 2.5D system that shipped in Horizon Zero Dawn and Forbidden West (the HZD floor we must match). It adds three things to Nubis Evolved 2022: the two profile LUTs at full resolution, before/after erosion posters, and one consolidated lighting equation. Slides 47–194 describe the voxel system shipped in Burning Shores. Clouds are grown with a fluid sim and composited in Houdini ("frankenclouding"). They are stored as a coarse 8 m **dimensional profile** (an SDF-derived 0→1 gradient) plus **detail type** and **density scale**. At render time the profile is **up-rezzed** by eroding it with a 128³ four-channel noise. The wispy noise is Curly-Alligator and the billowy noise is Alligator, each blended low→high frequency over the profile itself. A "twice-folded" octave is added within 150 m. The deck claims 0.5 m effective precision from 8 m voxels. Lighting uses the profile as a probability field for multiple scattering and ambient, plus a 256×256×32 summed-density light grid. The march sphere-traces a BC1-compressed SDF with an adaptive step floor of 1 m. For us the lesson is plain: **surface detail comes from the up-rez, not from the authored data**. Our voxel sampler is already a faithful port of it. Our gaps are art-bias defaults, the step size, the 2.5D profile model (no LUTs, no HZD erosion law) and authoring operations.

## 2. Key ideas (deduplicated)
**2.5D (HZD/HFW) model**
- Vertical-profile method: 5 NDFs (min height, max height, coverage, bottom type, top type). `vertical_profile = topLUT(top_type,h) × bottomLUT(bottom_type,h)`, then `× coverage` [s18]. The LUTs are 64×64 images, sampled into CSVs. Tops rise **in steps** and are not monotone, giving tiered shoulders. Bottom type 0 gives a crisp base and type 1 a long soft underside [s18; §7a].
- The envelope method is for orographic and fly-through cloud. Its profile is `top^1.5 × bottom^2 × edge(0..35 m)` over min/max height NDFs [s19–20]. It sits below the peaks, under the vertical-profile shell [s14].
- HZD noise: low-frequency Perlin-Worley plus three Worley octaves, combined per model into "wispy" and "billowy" composites. These are blended by type [s22–23].
- HZD erosion is **additive**: `saturate(noise − (1 − profile))`. High noise means more cloud, and noise stays visible at full strength in the core [s24]. The voxel era replaced it with `ValueErosion` [s107].
- Pseudomotion only: the noise scrolls with the wind and the NDFs are not advected [s25, s91, s173].

**Voxel up-rez (the detail engine)**
- Store an 8 m profile (from the SDF, so there is always an outside→inside gradient), detail type and density scale. Up-rez per sample, trading memory for ALU [s76–80]. A plainly voxelized Tallneck mesh renders as a billowy cloud, so the billows come from the up-rez [s66, s78].
- The physical rule: falling density gives curly, layered wisps; rising density gives layered billows [s83–88].
- New noises: Alligator (billows with cloud-like lacunarity) and Curly-Alligator (inverted Alligator distorted by curl). They replace inverted Worley ("packed spheres") and Perlin-Worley [s89–90].
- Frequency is blended **over the profile**. Wisps go LF→HF linearly in P. Billows go LF→HF over `P^0.25` and are capped at 0.3 [s95, s98] (see §7c on the narration).
- Near the camera, the fetched HF channels are folded twice (×4 apparent frequency) and blended in over 50–150 m. The fold costs no extra fetch [s102–104, s109].
- After erosion: multiply by `S^4`, then sharpen with an exponent below 1. A low S softens the fringe. Within 50–150 m the result is softened and capped [s107, s109].
- The noise MIP rises with distance (~15% faster). The noise tiles every 100 m [s92].

**Lighting that reveals form** (lighting is parked, but these terms separate lobes and layers)
- MS: the profile serves as an in-scatter probability, with Beer–Lambert relaxed toward the sun and deep inside. This gives dark edges and an inner glow [s116–125].
- Ambient is `sqrt(1−P)`. In the voxel era it is multiplied by `exp(−summed density toward the sky)`, which darkens undersides and keeps crowns bright [s30, s126–129].
- Light grid: two in-line samples, then a 256×256×32 summed-density grid. It is 40% faster and gives long inter-cloud shadows [s113–115].

**March**
- Sphere-trace the SDF outside the cloud and use the adaptive step `max(1, 0.08√t)` inside. Jitter moves only the sample, and is animated within 250 m. Sample only where SDF < 0 [s138, s148–150].
- The SDF is BC1 with a round-down 565 scalar encoding (encoded ≤ true), close to 16-bit precision at 0.5 B/texel [s142–145].
- Near/far split at 200 m (480×270 / 960×540) with no temporal reconstruction. It is used only for 40/60 Hz; 30 Hz is one pass [s45, s157–160].

**Authoring and layering**
- Grow clouds with a fluid sim. The Aero clips start from a thin flat lens at the base height [s63–64]. Composite them in Atlas (sources: voxels, points, meshes, 2.5D data; edits: cutouts, erosion, squashing) [s65].
- Use one dense world grid of 512×512×64 at 8 m (4 km × 4 km × 512 m). Sparse grids were rejected for per-sample indirection cost and schedule [s67–73].
- Hero-first workflow:
  - Sketch the E–W and N–S views against the sun arc [s162].
  - Place one big cloud and check it from the ground at every time of day. It is built from 4–5 rotated sub-boxes.
  - Add a flat, wispy **skirt** as connective tissue, then tunnels from mesh paths [s163].
  - Repeat per formation to get a coastline chain, inland heaps and an isolated offshore cloud [s167].
- Stormbird: sim small, then scale up and frankencloud. The interior is lit by lightning. It dissipates by a scripted method that the deck does not explain [s179–186, s176].
- The takeaway is perceived visual complexity, not voxel resolution [s173 notes].

**(a) Bottom type 0: crisp or ragged?** Range note 001-046 says crisp; `ne22.md:31` and `hzd-nubis-evolved.md:72` say ragged.
- **Resolution: type 0 is the crisp, flat base.**
- s18 bottom CSV: column 0 rises 0 → 0.33 → 0.60 → 0.73 → 0.80 over rows 16–24, while column 63 rises 0 → 0.07 → 0.27 → 0.47 → 0.53.
- NE p.26's table shows the same thing: the left side is bright right down to its floor, the right side fades darkly over a long span.
- With the s24 additive erosion, a profile of 0.8 just above the floor keeps nearly all the noise, which gives a filled, flat base. A slow ramp lets only noise peaks survive, which gives a ragged base.
- The NE p.27 still ("Bottom Type = 0.0") shows smooth, flat undersides on the main masses; the ragged bits are distant fringe.
- Our generator agrees: a bottom of 6–8 m reads as a "flat cut" for cumulus (`generate/shaders.ts:10-11, 25-27`).
- Fix `ne22.md:31` and `hzd-nubis-evolved.md:72` (not edited here).

**(b) Noise byte size.** s22 (HZD noise) and s90 (voxel noise) carry the same label block: 4 channels, 128³, "Uncompressed, 2 Bytes / Texel, 4.194 MB". The number is internally consistent (128³ × 2 B), but 2 B can hold four channels only at 4 bits each.
- The pack's `NubisVoxelCloudNoise.*.tga` (RLE, 32 bpp) has 218–255 distinct values per channel per slice. Only 4–7% of the values are multiples of 17; 4-bit data expanded to 8 bits would be 100%.
- So the shipped noise is **RGBA8, 8.389 MB**. GPU Pro 7 also says 128³ RGBA (`sources/gp7.md:13`).
- **Resolution:** the label is a reused caption. Use RGBA8, as our `resources.ts:47` does.
- `nubis-sources.md:68` ("RGBA16, 4.2 MB") is wrong on both counts.

**(c) HF or LF at thin edges?** The notes on s93 (wisps: high frequency in the less dense regions) and s97 (billows: higher frequency near the surface) contradict the code on s95 (`lerp(r,g,P)`: LF at P = 0) and s98. The s98 notes themselves say edges should be rounder than the core.
- **Resolution: the code is authoritative.** It is shipped, identical in the pack, and matches the s95/s98 diagrams.
- Nuance:
  - Billows blend over `P^0.25`, and billow erosion only bites where P < 0.3. So the visible billow silhouette is already 56–74% HF; pure LF appears only in the faintest fringe. In effect, s97 describes what billows look like.
  - Wisps blend linearly, so their silhouette is LF-dominated and s93 does not match.
- Cheap A/B: swap the wisp blend (try-next #9).

**(d) Low/mobile near-detail gate.** **Real.**
- `shaders/march.wgsl.ts:218` sets `hfDetails = ts < frame.misc.x`. `misc.x` is `f[72] = settings.detailDistance` (`runtime.ts:856`; frame layout `shaders/frame.wgsl.ts:29`). Presets give 100 m (low) and 60 m (mobile) (`runtime.ts:73-74`).
- Inside the gate, `shaders/density.wgsl.ts:55-61, 70-73` apply the HHF blend and the soften `pow(d, lerp(.5,1,f))·lerp(.666,1,f)` with `f = (d−50)/100`. Outside the gate, neither is applied.
- The deck's ramps reach identity only at 150 m, so a gate below 150 m cuts them off mid-ramp:
  - at 100 m (f = 0.5): density 0.1 jumps from 0.148 to 0.1 and 0.9 jumps from 0.770 to 0.9;
  - at 60 m (f = 0.1): 0.197 → 0.1 and 0.660 → 0.9.
- The result is a jitter-dithered spherical shell of density discontinuity around the camera. Ultra, high and medium (150 m) are seamless.
- Fix: gate at `max(detailDistance, 150)` for the soften, or remap the 50/150 constants to `detailDistance`.
