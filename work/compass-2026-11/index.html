Clouds Audit Compass

The plan since the November audit. This replaces the phase list on the earlier Compass, which stays as the record of the work before the audit.

The goal

The best-looking, fastest cloud system that can run in the browser.

  • Looks are judged against reality: your photos, the simulated clouds you rate highly, and physically correct light from a path trace. Then your eye.
  • Speed is judged in milliseconds on the target machines.
  • Every source is a toolbox, not a target. Horizon and Nubis, Takram, Sky Pro, Frostbite, the papers and Houdini are places to take methods from. Horizon's clouds are one style; none of these is a gate.
  • Differing from a source is fine when the reason is better quality, shape or speed. That is not a reason to skip hard work, or to avoid a method because a source uses it.

No gate on this page compares against a Horizon frame, a Decima number or a published constant.

Three problems, three truths

The clouds looked wrong for three separate reasons that had been tuned through each other. Each now has its own reference, and no round mixes them.

LightA path trace of the same density, sun and sky. Compared in linear radiance by a scorecard.
ImageA converged march of our own lighting: fine steps, no history, many samples. Shows what sampling alone costs.
ShapeYour photos and the simulated clouds you rated highly, by silhouette measures and then your eye, viewed under path-traced light.

What the rig has shown so far

  • The references are sound. Our pathtracer passes the furnace and absorber tests on real cloud grids and agrees with Cycles within 0.3 %. Clear-sky pixels of the two renderers match within 0.05 %.
  • One body from our procedural sky, path traced, looks like a plausible cumulus. At the scale of a single cloud the lighting is the larger fault, more than the audit first said.
  • Against your photos, our sky has too much fine fuzz on outlines and bases, too much flat underside and too little clear sky. Mid-scale lobes are inside the photo range. Body thickness is not yet measurable.
  • "Brightness is about right" was a tone-map effect. In linear light our lighting is 1.26× the reference at noon and 1.62× in thin fringes.
  • Backlit and sunset clouds are far too dark on every card cloud: thick bodies down to 0.24× of the reference.
  • Lit faces carry 2.2 to 2.9× too much fine texture against the path trace.
  • Perfect sampling removes only pixel-level speckle. At normal viewing size the converged frame looks nearly the same, so the shaving-cream read is shape and lighting.

Structure we are building toward

Guerrilla splits the job into a sky-wide procedural system, voxel hero clouds, and a generator with authoring on top. That split is a sound starting layout, so we begin from it and rebuild the two tangled parts clean. Any part can be replaced by something that looks or runs better.

Rules of the plan

  • One default path. Everything else stays in the code, frozen.
  • One fixed scene card; every round reports the same scorecard.
  • Agents measure. You judge, on a few sheets per phase. No model judges taste.
  • Each place we use, change or drop a source's method goes in DIVERGES.md with its measured effect. It is a record of choices, not a list of gaps to close.
  • One change per step. A failed gate is a result, not a reason to widen the change.
  • Every run has a time and a memory cap. Hours-long runs need your yes first.
