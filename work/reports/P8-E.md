# P8-E Sparse hero storage from WebVDB: design spike
Status: ready for review
Changed: `work/specs/P8-hero-vdb.md` (new), this report. Nothing else; WebVDB read-only. Scratch prototypes in the scratchpad (`p8e/measure-memory.mjs`, `p8e/web/bench.{html,js}`, `serve.mjs`, outputs in `p8e/out/`), not in the repo.
Verified:
- Memory: `node measure-memory.mjs` runs the repo's built importer (`dist/import.js`, fog mode, the shipped assets' options) on sim-cumulus, sim-congestus and the Disney quarter VDB at 8 / 4 / 2 m and counts occupied voxels, 8³ bricks, shell voxels and NanoVDB Fp8 node sizes (leaf 608 B, lower 33,856 B, upper 270,400 B from WebVDB's stride tables).
- GPU: `scripts/gpu/capture.mjs --url http://127.0.0.1:5188/bench.html` (headless Chrome 149, Metal, timestamp queries) on WebVDB's `wdas_cloud_quarter_fp8.nvdb`: a 256³ crop decoded through the vendored `pnanovdb.wgsl`, then 1920×1080 rays × N coherent samples through four kernels; per-kernel sums equal (mean 23.02 / 22.94 / 23.02), medians of 12 runs, kernels interleaved.
Measures (spec §2 has the full tables):
- Storage per 700 m hero: 4 m = 2.2–2.4 MB `fine.nvdb` (Fp8, profile only) vs 22–29 MB dense; 2 m = 13–14 MB vs 174–236 MB. Brick pool at 4 m: 3 MB (r8) / 12–13 MB (rgba8); 2 m: 20–22 / 70–90 MB; a 40 m shell saves 15–25%. Occupancy 22–27% of the crop box; 3,000–3,300 leaves at 4 m, 20–22 k at 2 m.
- Per-sample cost relative to one hardware trilinear fetch (today's `sampleModeling`): brick pool (indirection `textureLoad` + fetch) **1.16× / 1.20×** (1 and 4 voxels per step); NanoVDB Fp8 trilinear through the readaccessor **2.19× / 2.71×**; one nearest accessor tap 0.37× / 0.60×. GPU decode of the 16.7 M-voxel crop from the tree: 0.85 ms.
- Converter time (importer, node): 1.1–1.6 s at 4 m, 8.5–9.5 s at 2 m per hero.
Recommendation (spec §0, §3): NanoVDB Fp8 `.nvdb` as the hero wire format for the profile only (2–4 m), decoded at load into a brick pool (rgba8unorm, 10³ bricks with apron) plus an indirection grid over the regional volume; `cloudDensity` reads it through `sampleModelingHero` (one load + one fetch) in the march only. Detail type, density scale, SDF and the light grid stay at 8 m; the coarse profile under a hero is the mean of its fine voxels with an occupancy floor so the SDF remains conservative. The tree is not marched (2.2–2.7× per sample, 12 KB gzip WGSL against 4 KB of core headroom); its traversal is only an optional GPU decode later. Static heroes stamp by CPU re-bricking with snapped transforms and `replace` mode; movable heroes (≤ 4) get an instance atlas, a per-hero transform and SDF min (phase H5). Fine off on mobile/low tiers. Expected march overhead ≤ 3% without heroes on the ray, 3–5% inside; acceptance ≤ +10% / +0.3 ms at 1440p high.
WebVDB (spec §6): use `NanoVDBFile`, the CPU reader + a new leaf walker, and `buildFromVdb`/`buildFromDense`/`quantize('fp8')`/`writeNvdb` (native-parity tested); vendor with NOTICE until 0.1.x is published; upstream our parser changes (Blosc lz4, `select`, tiles, pre-222 leaves). Not needed: `three-nanovdb`, HDDA, `decodeToAtlas` (dense, three-specific). Optional later: a renderer-agnostic `pnanovdb_decode_bricks` compute.
Phasing (spec §7): H1 runtime overlay + test page, H2 converter + `vdb-to-nvdb.mjs`, H3 loader + static stamp, H4 in-browser importer/export, H5 instanced heroes, H6 tiers/art/captures/docs, H7 WebVDB upstreaming; each with acceptance measures.
Open issues:
- The GPU numbers are desktop Metal only; the phone path is "fine off by tier" until P8-D can measure. The 2.2–2.7× tree figure is for coherent rays with a warm accessor; the march's sun samples would be less cache-friendly, so it is a lower bound of the tree's disadvantage.
- `convertVdb` at 2 m needs about 1.2 GB of node heap (ran with `--max-old-space-size`); the in-browser 2 m import should stream or stay at 4 m.
- Spec §9 lists four owner decisions (4 m default, rgba8 vs r8 pool, publishing WebVDB, whether H5 is in scope).
- Not committed; BOARD untouched (task line stays `[~]` for the orchestrator to flip).
