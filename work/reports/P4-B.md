Findings (pack = parkouring + stormbird, SDF = EDT of profile > 0, depth = -sdf):
- Median profile vs depth is close to linear: about 0.01 at 4 m, 0.1 at 12 m, 0.2-0.28 at 20 m, 0.35-0.37 at 28 m, then 0.58 (parkouring) / 0.4-0.49 (stormbird) at 36-56 m. So the legacy `d/96` median was not far off; the real differences are (1) a very wide spread at fixed depth (p10 0.075 to p90 0.5 at 20 m), so the interior is a patchwork of mid values rather than a plateau at 1, which is what makes erosion `sat((P - noise)/(1 - noise))` carve the interior, (2) a lifted/thin floor (profile at the lowest 20% of height is 0.1-0.4 of the mid-height value at the same depth), and (3) strong height dependence of the other two channels. The old mapping reached P = 1 in sphere interiors and was smooth, hence soft spheres.
- Detail type is mostly a function of height: 0 below ~15-30% height, about 0.5 mid, 1 above ~80%; deep voxels read wispier (stormbird p50 0 at depth 28+ m for h < 0.5), near-surface billowy.
- Density scale rises with height: parkouring 0.02 to 1.0 by h = 0.6; stormbird 0.09 to 0.5 at the top. Nearly depth independent.
Fit (`PACK_FIT` in nvdf_math.py): profile = sat((d_eff - 1.4)/66)^1.05 * (0.1 + 0.9 smoothstep(0, 0.22, h)), d_eff = d * exp(0.64 * n), n = unit low-frequency noise (3 voxel correlation); detail = smoothstep(0.1, 0.75, h - 0.25 sat(d/32)); scale = 0.04 + 0.71 smoothstep(0, 0.6, h). Profile parameters from a 400 step random search against p10/p50/p90 per 4 m depth bin plus a height check (mean abs error 0.09); detail and scale were searched too but the two clouds disagree (parkouring reaches scale 1, stormbird 0.5), so those are hand-set compromises (loss 0.20 detail, 0.38 scale: expect the fit to sit between the two clouds, not match either).
