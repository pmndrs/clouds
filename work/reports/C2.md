# C2 Horizon haze and aerial perspective
Status: ready for review

Outside the listed files: `frame.wgsl.ts` (a `haze` vec4: 368 to 384 bytes), `sky-layer.ts` (sky depth textures rg32float to rgba32float), and one source row in `docs/research/README.md`.
Diagnosis:
- **AP was already applied once, at a weighted depth.** The march and both sky-layer parts weight by `T * (1 - stepT)`, then do one AP-LUT lookup. The composite `scene * T + cloud` already is H16 eq. 22: the sky's in-scatter behind the cloud is cut by `T`. So nothing in the structure was missing.
- **Why the far clouds did not fade.** An AP debug view (contours of LUT transmittance) gave 0.8–0.9 at 20–30 km. The default Earth air of @pmndrs/sky is very clean (about 200 km visibility). Takram's horizon comes from an extra exponential haze (`approximateHaze`, 3e-5/m, 1 km scale height) on top of its AP. Read in its installed source.
- **Why the horizon was a bright, speckled band.** The sky layer passed `microOcc = 1`, so the glow of distant clouds was never occluded, and its six cheap sun samples reached only 470 m. At a 4° sun, every far cloud lit up like a rim: a bright cream band of sub-pixel highlights.
Does:
1. **Horizon haze** (`hazeDensity` 0.05/km, `hazeHeight` 1000 m, `hazeBase` 0). It is applied once per ray at the weighted depth, after AP.
   - It uses the analytic optical depth of the exponential layer (the same form as the sky's own fog).
   - The cloud fades toward the sky-view-LUT radiance in that direction (H16 p.22: fog takes the LUT colour), so a fully hazed cloud equals the sky behind it and clear sky is untouched. No literal colours.
2. **Sky-layer glow occlusion.** `microOcc` comes from the smooth field 8 m toward the sun. A first try with the up-rezzed density aliased into a herringbone pattern at quarter resolution (species midday), so it was dropped.
3. **Far-field sun samples** stretch with `1/sin(elevation)`, from 1x at 45° up to 4x at 10° and below. Same sample count.
4. **Depth² (Ghost of Yōtei).**
   - The sky layer also stores the opacity-weighted mean and spread of the whole ray, carried through the temporal resolve.
   - The composite keeps the share of cloud in front of a surface (a linear stand-in for the normal CDF over ±1.25σ) instead of an all-or-nothing test at one mean distance.
5. `lightDebug = 6` gives every light term without AP or haze.

- **Twilight sky (problem 2).** C1's diagnosis was wrong.
  - @pmndrs/sky 0.5 has ozone (Hillaire/Unreal tent, 6.5e-4 / 1.88e-3 / 8.5e-5 per km) and a multiple-scattering LUT.
  - Normalised hues toward the sun at −2°: horizon #FF5D29, 5° #FFA06E, 15° #FFBCEA, 25° #E8ACFF, 40° #D08BFF, 60° #AA6AFF. That is the photo ramp (pink, mauve #927D97 ≈ #F6D3FF normalised, purple, indigo).
  - At +3/+4° (the dusk and sunset bookmarks) the sky above 25° is still blue, which is correct before sunset. The purple band appears at −1 to −4° and is only dark at a fixed exposure.
  - No fix and no proposal for the sky package are needed. Cloud bodies at −1° are mauve (`species-neg1`).
- **Stipple (problem 3).**
  - It is not the voxel jitter (static versus animated far jitter: identical) and not a JPEG artefact (it is in PNG too).
  - It comes from the sky layer: off (`sky=0`) it drops from 0.40 to 0.10, without temporal accumulation it rises to 0.69, and without jitter it is 0.65. It is quarter-resolution sampling noise of the analytic field that the 16-frame history (blend 0.3) only partly averages.
  - C2 lowers it 0.47 to 0.37 (PNG band-pass metric, −1° view).
  - Measured but not adopted: blend 0.15 (−13%, slower response), half step (−5%), lighting every step (−8%).
