Where the cost is (`timing.txt`):
- **The sky layer is latency-bound.** At 1280x720 it costs 1.86 ms, against 2.3-2.6 ms at 1440p with four times the rays. The longest rays in each workgroup set its time: the horizon band, up to 256 steps.
- In paired A/B, only the per-step view sample matters:
  - Worley billow off: x0.72-0.79. One octave: x0.82.
  - Profile noise off beyond 6 km: x0.93.
  - Light refresh x4: x1.0. No cheap sun/up samples: about x1.0. So the lighting is not the cost.
  - Workgroups as 32x2 rows: x1.0. Far step growth: x0.95-1.0.
- So RDR2's 1 ray per 2x2 tile would buy little here. It cuts rays, not the serial work per ray.

Kept (A/B-identical or approximations judged invisible):
1. **The light samples no longer run the Worley octaves.** `select(billowGrad(..), bGiven, useGiven)` evaluated both operands, so every in-line sun sample ran the full billow it was meant to reuse. It is now a branch (exact).
2. **Worley F1 pruning**: own cell first, then a neighbour only if its cell box is nearer than the best point (exact).
3. **Billow reach bound.** The billow is at most 0.64, so `K <= kMax` and the surface is no higher than `x_s(kMax)`. Below that the sample is empty without the octaves, and the step skips to that level. Before, the step used the global -0.46 that assumes the largest billow anywhere. The empty/full decision is exact; only the skip lengths change.
4. **The in-line sun samples reuse the view sample's profile noise and patch** (36-48 m features, samples 8-32 m away). The tower shift is still evaluated.
   - Result for items 1-4: the species page is deterministic (HEAD against itself: 0.01/255). The sky-layer areas differ by sampling only: `mixed-side.jpg`, mean 0.84/255, and I see no difference.
5. **Dome:** the sky part's light is re-evaluated every 24 m + 3% (as the layer does), and its 6 sun + 3 up samples are merged pairwise into 3 + 2 over the same segments. Dome time x0.68. The reflections in `water-shore.jpg` read the same; waves animate, so a pixel diff means nothing there.
6. **Wall column (fixed).** The light grid's sun march stopped at the box face. Voxel cloud next to a wall the sun shines through was lit as if the field beyond were empty.
   - The build now continues each ray through the sky layer's cheap field past the box (`lgOutside`, up to 24 steps). It goes into a new `.b` channel and invalidates when the layer's window changes.
   - The march and dome tap it with `lightGridSampleClamped` (`.r + .b`, face-clamped). The shadow map keeps `.r` only, because it marches the outside field itself (otherwise the outside part is counted twice).
   - The reach matches the layer's own sun samples: 470 m times `1/sin(el)`, up to 4x. An 8 km reach turned a straddling tower's voxel half grey.
   - In `wall-stratocumulus.jpg` the column is gone and both sides match. A 1 px seam line remains; it was also there in HEAD.

Open issues:
- **Mixed is over budget at horizon (3.71) and ground (3.47, at the line).** The next levers are all quality or structural decisions for the owner:
  - billow and noise LOD at distance (-0.2 to -0.4 ms on the sky layer);
  - splitting the horizon rays, which are the tail, over two frames;
  - a cheaper billow, such as a baked tiled Worley texture: the octaves are the sky layer's main cost.
- **Review `mixed-horizon-tower.jpg`.** A congestus tower standing across the wall now shows its voxel half in the shadow of its sky-layer half, with a diagonal terminator and the voxel field's bubbly shadow texture. It is consistent (both sides see about 470 m toward the sun), but it is a visible change. `LG_OUT_REACH` in `light-grid.wgsl.ts` is the knob.
- **The low-light sky-layer stipple is not done.** Because the layer is latency-bound, the cheapest fix is probably more fresh samples per cycle: 2 per 4x4 block per frame, or 2 jitter sub-samples per traced pixel. This should cost far less than the 2x the ray count suggests. C2's other levers (blend 0.15, -13%) trade response.
- **Background spikes:** a light-grid rebuild dispatch (1/16 of the slices plus the outside march) measured 0.34-0.42 ms per frame, under the 1.7 ms limit. The first-ever build runs all slices at once; with the sky layer already live at that frame it is a startup spike, normally avoided because the layer bakes for about 87 frames first.
- **For S1 (lower rates):**
  - the dome refresh: 1/16 per frame; reflections tolerate 15-30 Hz;
  - the light-grid rebuild;
  - the shadow-map slices;
  - the sky weather bake (24 rows per frame);
  - the sky layer's own 1/16 trace, the biggest pass.
