## What it is
- **Asset** (`vdb_to_direct.py`, hython): the VDB density at its native voxel (CloudPack: 4 m, x2 finer than C1), never resampled. `density.bin` r16float = density / densityRef (p99); `sdf.bin` = EDT of density > 1e-4 (empty-space skipping only); `nvdf.bin` R = saturate(density / densityRef), G 0, B 1, A = framing mask. `--like <fog asset>` crops to exactly that asset's world box (alignment IoU vs the C1 mask 0.94 / 0.92, peak at zero shift) and copies its occupancy into A, so the flagship places and frames it identically (camera positions equal to C1's to 1e-5 m). CloudPack 02: 356x504x190, 273 MB on disk, ~250 MB VRAM; the EDT takes 5-6 min (pure numpy).
- **Runtime:** a volume with `direct` is marched by a separate march variant whose density is one trilinear fetch: no profile, erosion, noise, up-rez or near fade. sigma = density x `settings.directDensity` (1/m per unit VDB density, default 0.6 = V1's `density_mult`). The light grid, shadow map and dome integrate R (the same density), so `s.lightDensityScale=1` is the honest setting (1.2 compensates the up-rez on the NVDF path). Lighting is unchanged: 'nubis' gets saturate(density/p99) as its in-scatter weight (it has no profile); 'octave' needs none. NVDF path untouched: its shaders are byte-identical; C1 re-shoots differ by 0.0-0.3/255 mean.
- **Why binding 7:** a new binding takes the composite to 17 sampled textures (default per-stage limit 16, validation error). A direct volume has no fine overlay, and only the fine march variant reads the pool.

## Result: the foam is form, the rest is lighting
- **Form.** Rendered directly, 02 and 09 read as one cohesive vapour body with fine turbulent surface detail; C1 shows the same clouds as separated puffs with shrunken, eroded silhouettes. With identical lighting the only change is the density chain, so R1's cohesion problem is caused by the Nubis profile/erosion chain on these inputs, not by the renderer. Silhouette vs Karma (02 photo, IoU): C1 0.82, direct 0.93, ultra 0.96.
- **Remaining differences vs Karma** (02 photo, values relative to each frame's own sky patch; Karma = V1 `s05`, 960x540, 32 spp, a preliminary frame):

| | lit / sky | shade / sky | shade B/R | edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Karma | 1.43 | 0.85 | 1.07 (neutral grey, warm cast) | 1.90 |
| direct nubis + Karma HG (dk) | 1.65 | 0.98 | 1.65 (blue) | 0.99 |
| same at ultra (du) | 1.65 | 0.98 | 1.65 | 1.82 |
| direct octave (do) | 1.57 | 0.91 | 1.38 | 0.82 |

  All of these are lighting or resolution: (1) **our shade is sky-blue where Karma's is neutral grey.** Our shadowed body is lit mostly by the blue sky ambient. In Karma it is lit by multiply-scattered sunlight; octave is closer (1.38). This is the gap the R1 judges named. (2) **Our lit-to-shade contrast is lower and the lit side brighter relative to the sky.** Part of this is the sky models (pmndrs vs Karma Physical Sky, turbidity 3: Karma's sky is greyer and its sun warmer) and V1's exposure fit, so judge it on the final frames. (3) **Edges are softer at `high`.** Ultra matches Karma's edge measure (1.82 vs 1.90), as R1 found. The phase function makes almost no difference (dn vs dk within 0.01). The 'nubis' lighting needs a profile: on a saturated 0/1 density (A2) its shade goes dark blue. Use 'octave' or a real profile for such assets.
