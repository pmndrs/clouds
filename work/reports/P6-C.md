- Read of the time-lapse: cumulus (0) -> congestus with turrets (30) -> a leaning cauliflower column that widens upward (60) -> the column under a thick-rooted anvil that spreads downwind, overhanging the tower, with the overshooting turrets poking through its top (90, 120). Flash pair: a hot white-warm core inside the column that fades up the tower and cools toward blue at the edge; the far side and the anvil stay dark. Without the flash the tower is a dusk silhouette.
Approach (why a 3D field, not a sim): the weather map is 2.5D (one [base, top] per column), so P6-B's cell could only extrude. The cell is now `stormField` in the shape stage (`shaders.ts STORM_WGSL`), a parametric 3D field in the generator's metres unioned (smooth max) with the cumulus field, so it has real overhangs and the EDT gives it a proper depth profile. Pyro keyframes were not used: a fixed set of keyframes cannot follow the P6-B life cycle continuously, and the parametric cell is free at runtime (one evaluation per voxel inside its bounding cylinder, amortized like the rest of the generation).
- Tower: a leaning core plus eight turrets, each a column widening toward its own rounded top (0.55-1.15 of the cell height) and spreading outward with height; radial law 1 - (r/R)^1.4 so the billow displaces the surface further (bigger lobes); soft base ramp (3 percent of the height). Anvil: a plume carried downwind (upwind edge 1.4 R, downwind reach AR, ragged rim), thick root over the tower thinning to a plate that keeps about four voxels and fades in density toward the rim (a geometric wedge terraces at 16 m voxels), 900 m undulation plus a 130-300 m ripple, mammatus pouches on the underside, billowy-typed and 0.7x density. The low field is suppressed under the cell. Life cycle unchanged (P6-B curves); `stormCellState()` is the TS mirror the demo uses for lightning placement (tested).
- API: `VolumeGeneratorOptions.storm` / `restart({ storm })` take a `StormCellState` (centre in generator metres, phase, sizes); `createEvolvingCloudscape({ storm })` builds it per generation from the P6-B `StormCellOptions` (+ `baseHeight`). `ProceduralWeatherOptions.storm` is now a boolean (the storm scene's low field); the weather uniform shrank 160 -> 128 bytes. The generator params block grew 96 -> 144 bytes (`PARAM_BYTES`, exported; stamp.ts binds it).
- Dimple lattice, root cause (two parts, both fixed at the source): (1) the profile pass's depth noise was one octave of axis-aligned value noise on a 3-voxel lattice (48 m at 16 m voxels); value noise has its extrema on the lattice points, so the trilinearly sampled NVDF showed a regular dimple grid on every smooth flank. Now `profileNoise`: three octaves in rotated frames, none finer than 2.5 voxels, same spread. (2) With `p_profileSigma=0` a polka-dot lattice remained: the billow's inverted Worley F1 is one identical cone per cell, so its finest octave tiles any smooth surface with same-sized, evenly spaced pebbles (also the stratocumulus pebbling P7-F noted). Now `worleyLobes`: one rounded bump per feature with its own radius (0.75-1.3 cells) and height, max over the neighbourhood (mean 0.41, spread 0.2 as before). Not the noise mip or the texture tiling for the flanks; the 100 m tiling of the run-time detail noise did show on a thin, wispy, uniform anvil plate (honeycomb), which is why the anvil is billowy-typed with a boosted profile depth.
- Lightning: `flashGlow` keeps the Nubis wisp term; the core term is inverse-square with a 110 m core, occluded by the cloud between the sample and the strike (three cheap NVDF profile taps along the segment, relaxed by 0.05 so e^-2 through about 400 m of solid cloud) instead of the local-density guess, and the colour runs from warm white at the strike (flashColor * (1.25, 0.98, 0.72)) to the flash colour at the edge. The glow is brightest at the strike and reaches the surface where the cloud is thin: shape instead of wash.
- Storm demo: bookmarks lit from azimuth 100 (side light; P6-B's 150 lit the tower flat), mature height 1560 m (turrets reach 1.15x), `?p_<param>=` overrides, `?height= ?radius= ?anvil=`, new `anvil` bookmark.
Reference (not committed): the textbook isolated cumulonimbus incus seen from a few km at ground level, as in the NOAA/NWS photo library shots over the Great Plains: a broad, flat, dark rain-free base; a column of several cauliflower turrets that leans and widens upward; at the top a flat-topped anvil spreading mostly downwind with a sharp upwind edge, a thick curved underside near the column and a thinning, fibrous downwind rim; an overshooting dome above the anvil over the updraft. `timelapse-distant.jpg` at 90/120 s has each of these except the fibrous rim (the rim fades but is not fibrous at 16 m voxels).
