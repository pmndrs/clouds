# P9-E2 Cirrus look pass: brush strokes, not wood grain
Status: paused for owner review (work in progress; board stays `[~]`)
Changed: `packages/runtime/src/cirrus/bake.wgsl.ts` only (the noise tile bake). Render, index, march, sky layer and dome are untouched.

Done (item 1, mostly):
- The streaky (r) and wispy (g) channels are rebuilt as explicit **brush strokes** (`strokes()` in the bake): one candidate stroke per cell, a
  16-segment curved centre line (heading spread plus random curvature), a width that tapers to points around a random swell, a feathered edge
  (eroded by coarse noise and pushed out along the fibres), a slow brightness variation along the stroke, and fibres that run along each
  stroke's own centre line (anisotropic fbm, uneven spacing). Strokes merge as a soft union, and the sky between them is open.
  - Streaky = a few broad sweeps (16–40 km long, 1–3.6 km wide) plus medium strokes (5–14 km) with 22 % hooks.
  - Wispy = shorter, wider, more curved and fanned strokes.
- The fine channel (a, read at 4x) is now low-contrast anisotropic fbm with no ridges. The old evenly spaced ridges were the global
  wood-grain lines.
- Seam fixes found by dumping the tile (scratch page `work/captures/scratch/p9e2/dump/`, loaded via `/@fs`):
  1. Duplicate wrapped cells (with 4 cells per tile, a ±2 search visited one stroke twice): candidates now sit at unwrapped cell positions.
  2. Hard edges where the nearest point of a curled stroke jumped between parts: each segment scores its own width-normalised distance, and
     the local coordinates are a soft-max blend. The result is continuous.
  3. Flat-capped hook heads: the head now has a round cap sized by its width, and the curl is spread over more of the stroke.
