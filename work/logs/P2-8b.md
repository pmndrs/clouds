## Read (against the photo)
- Outline: better. At noon tower4 is a tapering tower with a clear stepped shoulder on the right, no longer a straight-sided bucket; the top is rounded. At low it tapers too, but the top third is now a narrow finger, thinner than the photo's broad upper tower: the taper (rTop 230) is probably too strong. Still squatter overall than the photo, whose tower is taller against its base.
- Billow sizes: better. The lit face at low no longer reads as even popcorn; larger rounded billows with finer texture on them. But they are soft and mushy rather than the photo's crisp, high-contrast cauliflower, and the 340 m billows still do not break the outline into big domes the way the photo's do.
- Crisp tops: mostly kept at noon; at low the tower's upper left edge is a little ragged / fuzzy (wisps or fringe near the top).
- Silver lining: sunward has a bright uneven rim, widest along the bottom and lower right, as P2.8a's fringe; the upper edge stays thin. Still narrower than embergen02's.
- Noon side billows: not milky (the 25 m / x0.45 side fringe fixed P2.8a's airbrushed look).
- Worse: the base at noon is a wider, flatter, softer slab (the full low fringe plus the flanks), and the surface overall is less crisp than tower3 (the smaller 130 / 50 m pushes take out some definition along with the pebbles).
- Proposed next (one at a time): (1) ease the taper (rTop ~320 m) and make the core taller (~2.8 km) so it is a broad tower, not a finger; (2) sharpen the billows: keep the 340 m push but give the 130 m pass back its strength on up-facing lit surfaces only (crisp cauliflower on tops, calm on sides); (3) narrow the low fringe under the flanks at noon.
