# R1 blind judging, Opus (2026-10-10)

Judge: Opus, blind (no key, captures, reports or board opened). Rubric v2, all 8 criteria, 1–10, harsh scale: 9–10 = could pass in the owner's photos.
Set: `scratchpad/blind-r1/{photo,detail}/*.jpg`, 10 ids, each viewed at full 1920×1080 plus 1:1 side-by-side crops of suspected variant pairs.

References studied first: `santasSleigh/end.jpg`, `santasSleigh/detail/DSC00633, DSC00638, DSC00642`, `Frog Cloud/DSC00589-Pano.jpg`, `Frog Cloud/detail/DSC00565, DSC00575, DSC00584`.
What the references show, and what I scored against:
- Lit tops are cauliflower crusts: many small crisp billows on a soft, light-filled body. Edges are sharp in the sun and loose only on thin parts and undersides.
- The interior is vapour. Light goes into it, and lobes merge through a soft grey gradient with no hard seams. Bellies are warm grey and flat-bottomed.
- Each body is one mass. Satellites are separate small clouds with their own flat bases, not debris around the main body.

## Reasons

### r63: tall cumulus with an overhanging head, dense, sharp render
1. Lobe hierarchy 6: big head, body and right shelf carry mid billows. The step below that is pitting, not smaller domes.
2. Silhouette 6: a believable heaped tower with an overhang. The base is ragged and not flat, and the head is a bit mushroom-like.
3. Edges 6: lit rims are fairly crisp with a fine fringe, and undersides go soft. The crust is cottony, not cauliflower.
4. Shading 7: the best depth in the set. The crevices under the head and on the left flank are blue-grey and lobes read as separate volumes.
5. Overall 5: reads as a cloud at photo scale. In the detail view the porous, pock-marked surface gives it away.
6. Form 5: one convincing mass. The surface looks eroded, like a sponge, rather than built from billows.
7. Material 5: the lower right lets light in and reads as vapour. The pitted upper surface reads as foam or sponge.
8. Cohesion 6: one body with soft connective density in the lower right. The neck between head and body is a little pinched.

### r60: same cloud, lighter treatment, sharp render

3. Edges 5: everything is fluffy at the same softness, with no crisp lit crusts and a faint grain on the fringes.
4. Shading 4: lit almost evenly white. Crevices are faint and the shadow under the head is the only real depth.

## Variant groups

Three source clouds. Two of them come in four variants on two axes: a density treatment (lighter vs denser) and a render path (sharp vs soft, where the soft path is blurred and has a checker/dither fringe on its edges).

| Cloud | lighter, sharp | lighter, soft | denser, sharp | denser, soft | Best |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A, tall cloud with overhanging head | r60 | r14 | **r63** | r80 | r63 for form and depth, r60 for material |
| B, wide oval | r79 | r18 | **r81** | r48 | r81 |
| C, small broken | **r62** | r16 | – | – | r62, weak either way |

- **The sharp render path beats the soft one in every pair** (r60>r14, r63>r80, r79>r18, r81>r48, r62>r16). The soft path removes the only crisp edges these clouds have and adds a dither fringe. It never helps material: blurred dense variants turn into marshmallow (r48, r80), not vapour.
- **The density treatment trades material for structure.** The lighter treatment is more vapour-like and whiter, but it loses self-shadowing (A) or breaks into popcorn with holes (B). The denser treatment gives depth and the best cohesion (r81, r48 are the most continuous bodies), but its cavity walls drift toward whipped cream and the surface pits look like sponge (r63).
- Neither treatment yet gives the reference look: crisp cauliflower crusts over a soft vapour body with grey flat bellies. The likely answer is between the two. Keep the dense treatment's connective fill and shadowing, and get the lit crusts from sharp small-scale billows instead of from pitting.
- Cloud C is weak whatever the variant. A debris ring around a smeared core is a source or authoring problem, not a rendering one.

## Notes for calibration

- I deliberately scored material and cohesion as heavily as lobe detail, per `results.md`. That is why r60 sits level with r63 overall even though r63 is technically stronger.
- If the owner weights vapour softness most (as with c51 in round 1), expect them to put r60 first. If they weight depth and cohesion, expect r63 or r81.
- Blue cast in the shadows: every cavity is sky-blue, where the references' bellies are neutral to warm grey. This adds to the "not a photo" read on all ten.
