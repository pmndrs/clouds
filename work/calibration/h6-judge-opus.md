# H6 blind judging: Opus (2026-10-10)

Set: `blind-h6/` (8 images, 1920x1080), judged at full resolution with crops. I did not open the key, captures, reports or board.
References: GPU Pro 7 Fig 4.13–4.16, HZD 2015 in-game slides (p36–p39), HFW image55/56, and the owner photos (Santa's Sleigh `end.jpg`, Frog pano).
Scale: harsh (a 10 could pass in the owner's photos). Criteria: 1 lobe hierarchy, 2 silhouette, 3 edge, 4 shading/depth, 5 overall realism, 6 realism of form, 7 material, 8 cohesion, 9 HZD likeness.

## Pairs

Each pair shares the weather layout. The bodies sit in the same places.

| above (camera over the layer) | g43 | g94 | **g43** |

All four pairs change the same way. Version A has a faint low-density veil of wisps between bodies, less coverage, a clear sky gap above the horizon, and rounded tops from above. Version B has more mass, no veil, a carpet of popcorn running to the horizon, stacked internal shelves, and (from above) flat-cut mesa tops. **A wins all four**, by a small margin on ground and by a clear margin on the other three views.

## Owner's points, A vs B

- **Connective low-density flow:** A adds a visible veil of wisps between bodies (g28 upper left, g68 upper right, g24 top). It is the first time any of these renders shows sky-coloured, low-density vapour between the bodies. It is still a uniform haze layer, though, not skirts or trails that belong to particular bodies. From above (g43) it reads as grey smog lying over the ground.
- **Surface detail without foam:** A's distant tops have crisp billows. Two problems remain. The overhead and near bodies (g68) go smooth and plastic. The above view (g43) is single-scale bubble foam. Detached pellets float off the edges in both versions, and they are a CGI tell.
- **Internal terraces:** B shows them strongly (the plates in g37, the tonal steps in g70, the walls in g94). A reduces them, but the flat sheared base shelves remain in g28 and g68.
- **Flat-cut tops from above:** B (g94) shows obvious mesas. A (g43) fixes them.
- **Crowding toward the horizon:** B carpets the horizon. A leaves a clear sky gap and thins the field into haze. A is closer to HZD, although HZD still keeps more small distant clouds than A does.

## Three biggest gaps to HZD

1. **Lighting and material contrast.** None of the renders has HZD's bright sunlit crust over a darker interior, the darkening at lobe edges, or a large value range inside each body. The day shots are evenly lit in white and blue-grey. The sunset interiors are flat dark card with no internal glow.
2. **Detail placement and erosion scale.** There is one bubble scale on the tops (g43). The close and overhead bodies lose detail and go smooth and plastic (g68). The bases are flat horizontal shelves instead of the curl-distorted, inverted-Worley wispy bottoms in GP7 and HZD. Detached pellets float off the edges.
3. **Body-scale structure and cohesion.** The bodies still read as separate blobs with hard boundaries. The new veil is a uniform haze rather than flow that comes out of each body (trailing skirts, shredded edges, thinning satellites). Between the two versions, the coverage swings from too crowded (B) to sparse-but-isolated (A) without reaching HZD's mix of a few large bodies and many small, well-spaced ones.
