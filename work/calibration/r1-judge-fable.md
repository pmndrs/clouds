# R1 blind judge: Fable (2026-10-10)

Rubric v2 (8 criteria, 1–10). Blind: ids only, photo (48°) + detail (20°) per id, references re-studied first (Santa's Sleigh end + 3 details, Frog pano + 3 details). Calibration: 9–10 = could pass in the owner's photos; nothing here gets there. Scores are deliberately harsh.

What the references say that the renders mostly do not: the lit side is a crisp bright crust with only *subtle* tonal variation inside the lit mass (no hard blob-to-blob steps); crevices go slightly blue-grey, not dark; the belly is one large, smooth, grey, evenly lit sheet; thin edges go translucent rather than fuzzy; and lobes are bound into one body by low-density haze, with debris only as a thin trailing veil at the base, never as a spatter of hard mini-puffs.

## Reasons

## Variant groups

Three clouds, each rendered several ways. Within each group the density field is clearly the same (identical outlines and satellite placement); the variants differ on two axes: (a) how much fine detail survives (fuzzy/popcorn vs smooth large domes) and (b) how hard the surface is sharpened.

**Group A (r14, r60, r63, r80)** — tall body with an upper lobe leaning right, satellite row bottom-right.
- r14: fuzzy, flat, box artefacts. r60: same with crisper fuzz. r63: smooth big domes, deep crevices. r80: r63 blurred.
- **Best: r63.** Its volume and cohesion make it read as a cloud at photo size; the price is foam material. r80 removes the crust without adding vapour. r60/r14 are wool with artefacts. The ideal would be r63's large-scale form carrying r60's fine grain, with the grain modulated by a smooth low-density envelope rather than sitting on top as fuzz.

**Group B (r18, r48, r79, r81)** — wide round crown, flat base, lobe trailing left.
- r18: popcorn, soft. r79: popcorn, crisper. r48: smooth marshmallow domes. r81: large domes plus fine grain.
- **Best: r81, clearly.** It is the only render in the whole set with three scales of lobe and a real crevice, and it is the only one that would survive a glance in a photo. r48 shows the large-scale form alone (owner's "no inner ridges"); r79/r18 show the grain alone (popcorn). r81 is the combination, and the combination is what works.

**Group C (r16, r62)** — small humilis with a ring of detached puffs.
- r16: soft, translucent rims. r62: same with grain.
- **Best: r16, marginally.** Its edges are the most vapour-like in the set; r62's grain reads as noise. Both lose on the debris ring and on having no lit crust.

## Notes for the owner

- Material is the ceiling for everything here. Even r81 tops out at 5 on material because the big lobes are smooth meringue and nothing is translucent where it is thin. The two things the photos have that no render has: (1) a bright, *thin* lit crust with the lit interior only slightly darker and blue-grey crevices, i.e. light scattering *into* the lobe; and (2) thin edges that fade to sky rather than fuzz or hard rims.
- Cohesion is good in groups A and B (continuous body) and bad in C (spatter). The "harsh falloff between blobs" problem lives at the sub-lobe scale: in r18/r79/r60 every small blob has its own soft rim, so the surface reads as beads. r81 and r63 avoid this by having a smooth envelope and only varying the surface within it.
- Bellies: r48/r63/r81 darken; r18/r79/r16 stay nearly as white as the crown. The photo bellies are one smooth grey sheet. None of the renders has that sheet.
- Combination to pursue: r81's recipe (large smooth domes + fine grain + crevice) applied to group A's silhouette, with the fine grain damped on the belly and the thin-edge density allowed to go translucent.
