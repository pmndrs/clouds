### g28 (ground, open)
1. Lobe hierarchy 5: big bodies carry two or three sub-domes, then jump straight to a fine grain; the middle 3–4x step is missing.
2. Silhouette 6: domed tops, flatter bases, natural satellites; the top-right stratus slab is a hard-edged plate.
3. Edge 5: lit crusts fairly crisp, but a uniform stipple/dither grain on every surface reads as noise rather than metre-scale ridges; undersides are no softer than tops.
4. Shading 6: the big right body has a real shadow belly and crevice shading; overall contrast is low, lit side barely brighter than sky-lit side.
5. Realism 4: layout passes at a glance, grain and even lighting say CGI up close.
6. Form 5: reads as cumulus bodies; several are bread-loaf blobs.
7. Material 4: dense cotton; thin parts do not go translucent, they just get smaller.
8. Cohesion 4: islands on hard blue, almost no veil or low-density tissue between bodies. The owner's "too isolated" note stands.
- HZD 5: the closest layout in the set to Fig 4.16 (body sizes, spacing, thinning toward the horizon); missing the bright-crust/dark-belly contrast and wispy bottoms.

### g54 (ground, dense)
1. Lobe hierarchy 5: same body-to-grain jump; extra erosion makes everything one busy scale.
2. Silhouette 5: outlines more ragged, bodies fuse into masses; the slab in the top right has lumpy hard edges.
3. Edge 4: finely crinkled everywhere, the foam look; no top/bottom distinction.
4. Shading 5: more mid-tones from the thicker haze and a few darker bellies, but it flattens into grey-white mush.
5. Realism 4.

## Pair verdicts

- **Ground: g28 over g54, narrowly.** g28 keeps intact domed bodies, a readable lit crust and a shadow belly on the main body, and a Fig 4.16-like spacing. g54 does add the connective tissue the owner asked for, and that is the right direction, but it gets it by crowding and extra density, which brings back the fine-eroded foam surface and horizontal shelf striations at the bases. The owner may lean g54 for cohesion; I would not, because the cohesion costs silhouette and material.
- **Horizon: g68 over g37, clearly.** g68 has distinct bodies thinning into small puffs with sky between, like p036/p041. g37 is a popcorn carpet to the horizon with one texture scale and flat lighting.
- **Sunset: g24 over g70, clearly.** g24's rounded lobed silhouettes and selective rim light are the best shape and edge read in the set. g70 turns the bodies into stacked flat plates with visible terraces and a continuous band near the sun.
- **Above: g94 over g43, marginally.** g94 at least has separate bodies and a soft rounded dome on the right. It also exposes the biggest above-view flaw, flat ceiling-cut tops with vertical walls; g43 hides the same ceiling under a uniform cauliflower carpet that reads as suds.

Best single image: g24. Worst: g43.

## Three biggest remaining gaps to HZD

1. **Lighting and material.** Daylight renders are evenly lit cotton with low contrast and a uniform stipple grain; there is no bright crust over a darker interior and no light bleeding through thin edges. HZD frames, HFW image56 and the owner's photos all live on that crust-to-belly gradient. The sunset rim light (g24) shows the renderer can do selective edge brightening; the daylight views need the same contrast, plus translucency where density thins.
2. **Connective low-density flow without foam.** Open versions (g28, g68, g24) are islands with hard falloff; dense versions (g54, g37) connect bodies by crowding and fine erosion, which the owner reads as foam. HZD's answer is thin veils and wispy, curl-distorted bottoms around bodies that stay intact: low density, large scale, soft, with the blue still visible between bodies.
3. **Vertical structure.** Flat-cut tops at the ceiling with vertical walls (g94, and hidden in g43), stacked horizontal shelves inside bodies (g70, the bases in g54 and the right half of g68), and a hard-edged stratus slab in the top right of the ground views. Bodies should rise as domes under a soft ceiling and be flat only at the base, and the lobe hierarchy needs a middle step between body and grain.
