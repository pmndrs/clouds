# D1e blind judging (Fable), 2026-10-11

Set: `scratchpad/blind-d1e/` — full frames d01–d16 (1920x1080) and crops c01–c32 (2x zooms of 700x450-ish regions). Judged per the rubric's "Judging method": whole frames, the supplied crops, and my own 2x/4x crops of the same regions in all four images of a group (lit crust, shadow interior, undersides, satellites, shaded flanks). Key not opened. Grouping was done by cross-correlating the crops against the frames (crops are 2x zooms; offsets below), then visually.

Scores are 1–10 for *match to the reference* (not realism), on: (1) surface, (2) shading, (3) colour, (4) edges, (5) same cloud / same engine. Harsh.

Why those are the references: one image per group has a sub-lump scale (cauliflower florets a tenth of the lobe size, creases between them), a warm pink-grey shade side, a flat dark base band under the lit lobes, a white haze band at the horizon, and a soft video-capture look (the sky in c29/c02 even carries faint diagonal capture streaks). The other three in every group share one renderer: the same silhouettes to the pixel, the same lobe placement, and three post/noise variants of one engine.

The three variants are consistent across all four framings and I refer to them as:

- **G** (grainy): d01, d02, d16, d07 — blue-noise speckle on every silhouette, brightest whites, cool/blue shade, saturated sky.
- **W** (warm/flat): d11, d06, d09, d04 — smooth, warm grey, greyer sky.
- **S** (smooth/bright): d12, d10, d05, d08 — smooth, bright, blown-out lit crust, blue-ish sky.

## Hard artefacts per render

G (d01, d02, d16, d07):
- Speckle sawtooth on every silhouette, worst on small satellites (c32 satellite is a dot cloud, c17 whole outline).
- Bright speckled "light-leak" patches inside the shade (d01 top dome, d02 tower, d07 trunk).
- A thin vertical bright fibre down the lit crust of the A dome (c28 / A crust 4x).
- Dark blue blobs sitting "behind" lobes (A dome, C left dome, B tower shade) — a slab, not a crevice.
- Global blue cast and oversaturated sky; whites clip.

W (d11, d06, d09, d04):
- No sub-lobe scale at all: lobes are one soft mass ("cotton"), lit crust clips to flat white.
- Small angular lighter flecks in the shaded interior (A top: c27, A flecks 4x; also faintly in d12).
- Dark vertical slit in the D trunk (c09, d04 at ~(1120,760)).
- Lighter "window" patch in the C base shade (c24).
- Uniformly soft edges; thin parts and crusts have the same softness.

S (d12, d10, d05, d08):
- Same mush as W, plus the lit crust blooms past white (d12 worst, c08; d08 c18).
- Shade side goes olive/khaki (A) or dark blotchy brown slabs (C tower, c10).
- Same slit in the D trunk (c03), same flecks, same window (c23).

Reference-side caveats (do not copy): the ref frames are video captures — slight diagonal streaks in the sky (c29, c02), softness from compression, and in some groups the ref's satellites sit in slightly different places (A right shelf).

## Main remaining differences (what keeps every score at or below 5)

1. **Missing middle scale.** The ref has florets about a tenth of a lobe across with crisp creases between them; ours has the lobes and then either blue-noise grain (G) or nothing (W/S). This is the single biggest gap and it is what the owner sees up close.
2. **Shade side is a slab.** The ref's shade is a warm pink-grey, mottled with lighter lumps, with a distinct flat dark base band. Ours is a flat slab: blue (G), grey (W), olive/brown (S), with light leaks, flecks, windows, and in D a slit.
3. **Edge character.** The ref's crusts are crisp at the floret scale and only thin parts are soft. Ours is uniformly soft (W/S) or uniformly speckled (G).
4. **Colour / post.** The ref is warm pink-grey in shade, near-white on crusts without clipping, teal-grey skies with a white haze band at the horizon. G is too blue and clips whites, S blooms the crust and tints shade olive, W is closest but dull.
5. **Specific fingerprints** to remove regardless of look: the D trunk slit, the interior flecks/windows, the vertical fibre, the light-leak patches, the speckled silhouettes.
