# H7 blind judging: Fable (2026-10-10)

Judge: Fable, blind. Set: `scratchpad/blind-h7/` (8 images, 1920x1080, unlabelled ids). Rubric v2 (8 criteria) plus HZD likeness. References viewed: GPU Pro 7 p010-p013 (figs 4.13-4.16), HZD 2015 p036-p042, HFW image55/56, owner photos `santasSleigh/end.jpg` and `Frog Cloud/DSC00589-Pano.jpg`. Everything viewed at full resolution plus 2x crops of matching regions in each pair.

## Pairs

Identified by identical body layout; one variable changes per pair.

| View | ids | What differs |
|---|---|---|
| ground (camera low, looking up, horizon at y~570) | h35 / h69 | h35 has a wind-sheared streak veil laid across and between bodies; h69 is the same scene without it |
| horizon (horizon at y~680) | h39 / h59 | h59 has the streak veil (diagonal smears crossing the lit crowns); h39 clean |
| sunset | h42 / h66 | h42 has the streaks (vertical-diagonal smears between silhouettes, like motion blur or virga); h66 clean |
| above (over the deck, ground below) | h31 / h88 | h31 has streaky veil over the ground between bodies (reads as foam on water) with crisper body edges; h88 has soft fuzz localised to each body's skirt, no streaks |

The changed variable is the connective layer. In three of the four pairs it renders as a uniform, directional smear that crosses over tops as well as bases, which is exactly the "haze/smog" read from H6, not skirts coming off bodies. Only in the above view (h88) does the soft version read as skirts attached to the bodies.

## Pair verdicts

- **ground: h69 > h35.** h69's central body shows a real three-step hierarchy (body, ~1/3-scale lobes, fine grain) with contrast between crown and belly; h35 smears the same body with horizontal streaks that flatten the lobes and lower contrast across the whole field. The streaks cut over lit tops, so they read as cirrostratus or smoke drifting past, not as fractus under the cloud. Pellets along the lower band are identical in both.
- **horizon: h39 > h59.** Same story. h39 is the best single image of the set: a cluster of flat-based bodies at centre with satellites, pellets in the distance, enough gaps for depth. h59's diagonal smears cross the lit crowns and blur the lobe boundaries, and the connective tissue does not attach to any body.
- **sunset: h66 > h42, narrowly.** The silhouette view hides most of the material problems and h66's dark bodies with rim-lit edges and merged bases are the most HZD-like mood (cf. HZD 2015 p039). h42's streaks are the least offensive of the four because backlit smears can pass as virga, but they still cross the whole sky at one angle and look like motion blur over the bodies at top centre.
- **above: h88 > h31.** h88's fuzz hugs each body's skirt and underside, which is the first time the connective layer reads as attached, and the deck is a bit more cohesive for it. h31's veil over the ground is a streaky foam-on-water texture with crisp body edges sitting on top; it is the worst image in the set. Both are still a suds carpet from above: one bubble scale on top, no bigger domes carrying smaller ones.

Net: the streak layer should not ship as is in the ground, horizon or sunset views. The direction is right only where the fuzz is attached to a body and stays below its belly line.

## Specific notes on the asked-for items

- **Skirts vs haze**: the streak layer is a directional smear at one angle across the frame. It crosses lit crowns, does not attach to bodies and lowers contrast everywhere. It reads as cirrostratus, smoke or motion blur, not as fractus. Only the above view's soft variant (h88) has fuzz that hugs a body. A skirt should start at the belly line of a specific body, hang below or trail downwind of it, be curl-distorted, and never appear above the crown.
- **Lobe hierarchy**: present in the clean ground and horizon views (body, ~1/3 lobes, grain). Missing from above (one bubble scale) and from the distance pellets (one size). Grain is the same popcorn size on every body regardless of body size.
- **Shelves and terraces**: the left-hand lozenge bodies in the ground and horizon views have a flat horizontal cut line through the middle (a shelf inside the body). The centre tower in h39 has a terrace deck that reads as a plausible base skirt, which is the good kind. The top-right slab in both day views is a smooth, detail-less gray pancake with layered undersides: a different material from the bubbly bodies and the clearest "stratus slab" problem.
- **Overhead slab (from above)**: a suds carpet with one bubble size and no towers. From below, the slab is the top-right pancake. Both need to be built from the same bubbly material as the bodies, with a few domes breaking through.
- **Pellets at edges**: many small puffs along the lower band and in the distance, all roughly one size with the same stippled edge. Real humilis pellets (owner `end.jpg`) vary in size and have flat bases; ours are round dots. Count is too high in the horizon view.
- **Crowding**: the horizon view packs its bodies into one band (y 250-550) with little base-height variation, so the band reads as rows of similar bodies. Ground view is better. Sunset hides it.
- **Edge dither**: at 2x every edge shows a dotted stipple (march jitter or noise reconstruction). It makes all edges uniformly fuzzy and noise-torn. It is not a shape issue, but it costs the edge-character score on every image.

## Three biggest remaining gaps

1. **Material and lighting.** Evenly lit cotton with dithered edges: no crisp lit crust over a darker interior, no self-shadowed crevices between lobes, no translucency at thin edges. The sunset view only looks good because the sky does the shading. This is the largest distance to HZD p036/p037 and HFW image55, and it caps every daytime image at about 5 regardless of shape work. (Noted as blocked on the renderer baseline.)
2. **Connective tissue is still wrong in both variants.** The streak variant is a frame-wide directional smear that crosses crowns (haze/smog); the clean variant is islands with quick falloff. What is needed is fractus attached to each body: starting at the belly line, hanging or trailing downwind, curl-distorted, with tops left crisp. The above view's soft variant (h88) is the only hint of that and should be the model, not the streaks.
3. **Vertical structure and distribution.** A smooth featureless slab of a different material (top-right from below, the whole deck from above), flat cut lines through lozenge bodies, one grain size on every body, one pellet size in the distance, and a single crowded band with no base-height variation. Needed: the slab broken into the same bubbly material with domes pushing through, body-size-relative grain, a spread of pellet sizes with flat bases and fewer of them, and some bodies with higher bases so the band has depth.
