## Reasons

**c94**
- Hierarchy 6: the leaning head carries two clear generations of smaller domes (detail view shows ~3 scales on the cap), the best stacking in the set; the lower-right flank drops back to one scale.
- Silhouette 6: a real cumulus outline, tall head with a lower shoulder and a smaller satellite lobe on the right, but it leans as a whole and has no flat base.
- Edge 4: lit crusts are a little more defined than the rest of the set but still soft everywhere, with a visible stipple/dither at the boundary in the detail view.
- Shading 6: grey-blue bellies under the lobes and self-shadowed crevices on the head; lobes read as separate volumes.
- Overall 6: the only render whose photo framing reads as a cumulus at a glance; the softness and lean give it away.

**c61**
- Hierarchy 6: the main cap in the detail view has small domes riding a big dome, with a middle scale in between; the lower cluster is one scale.
- Silhouette 4: fragmented, a detached blob upper-left, a string of debris down the left side and a gap between the cap and the lower body; no base.
- Edge 4: soft overall with stipple at the boundary; crevice lines do show as slightly harder.
- Shading 6: good belly darkening and shadowed crevices; the cap reads as a lit dome.
- Overall 5: the best single piece of surface in the set, attached to a broken body.

## Notes

What separates the top from the bottom is almost entirely shape at the lobe level: c94 and c61 have small domes sitting on bigger domes with a middle scale between them, and lit faces that fall into grey crevices, so the eye reads stacked volumes; c39, c52 and c51 are either one bubble size repeated over a blob or no lobes at all, and the silhouettes are an egg, a scatter, and a cone. Shading tracks the same split: the better renders have a real blue-grey belly and self-shadow, the worst are near-uniform white.

What every render gets wrong against the photos: (1) no flat, dark base — every owner photo, including the small satellites, has a sharply flat grey underside, and no render has one; (2) edge character is uniformly soft, with a visible stipple/dither at the boundary in every detail view, whereas the photos' sunlit crusts are crisp over a few metres and only the wisps and undersides go soft; (3) the tonal range is compressed — the photos go from clipped white caps to mid-grey bellies with sky-blue fill on the shadowed sides, while the renders sit in a narrow near-white band; (4) satellites are detached popcorn or confetti floating around the body, while the photos' satellites are small flat-based cumulus humilis sitting at the same cloud-base altitude. None of the nine would pass in the owner's photos; the best is a convincing silhouette rendered in soft material, and the detail views are where all of them fall apart.
