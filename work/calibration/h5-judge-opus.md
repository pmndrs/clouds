# H5 blind judging: Opus (2026-10-10)

Set: `scratchpad/blind-h5/` (8 unlabelled 1920×1080 renders), judged at full resolution with 1:1 crops. Rubric v2 (8 criteria) plus a 9th score, **HZD likeness**. Key not opened.

References studied: GP7 Fig 4.13, 4.14, 4.15 and 4.16 (final shape); HZD 2015 in-game frames p038, p039, p043, p046, p086, p095; HFW cumulus (n3 image55, image56); owner photos Santa's Sleigh `end.jpg` and Frog `DSC00589-Pano.jpg`.

What HZD does that matters here: separate cumulus bodies with plenty of blue sky between them. Warm, bright, high-contrast sunlit domes, with powder-darkened crevices and grey flat bases. Small crisp billows only at the silhouette and on the top crusts. Bodies shrink smoothly toward the horizon and thin into haze. Edges are crisp on top and wispy below.

## Three biggest gaps to HZD

1. **Lighting and material.** HZD has warm, bright, high-contrast sunlit domes, with powder-darkened crevices and grey bases. Ours are cool blue-grey, lit evenly and low in contrast. Overhead bases show a smeared, marbled plaster texture, probably from curl-warped or too-low-frequency erosion at the base. Backlit, the interiors are flat cardboard grey with a neon rim stroke, when thin vapour should glow and thick cores should go dark (forward scatter and multiple scattering).
2. **Coverage and distribution over the sky dome.** Fig 4.16 and p095 show separate bodies with lots of blue between them, getting smaller toward the horizon and thinning into haze. Ours are too crowded. Overhead bodies are huge slabs that fill the frame, and the far field is a dense popcorn carpet or an unbroken band, not perspective-diminished bodies fading out.
3. **Detail placement and vertical profile.** HZD keeps crisp billows on the silhouette and top crusts and wispy bottoms, over a clean low-frequency body. Ours put mottled noise across whole interiors (h37, h66) and have no wispy bottoms. The aerial views expose the height profile: tops cut flat at the layer ceiling with vertical walls (h15), or density hanging downward in tendrils (h11), instead of rounded tops over flat bases.
