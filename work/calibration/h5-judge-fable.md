# H5 blind judgement (Fable, 2026-10-10)

Set: 8 unlabelled sky-wide procedural renders (`blind-h5/h11 h13 h15 h35 h37 h66 h71 h88`), judged at full 1920x1080 plus 960-px crops. Rubric v2 (8 criteria) plus a 9th, **HZD likeness**, scored against GP7 Fig 4.13–4.16 (Fig 4.16 cropped to full width), HZD 2015 in-game frames (slides 19, 36, 37, 43), HFW sunset/sunrays (`n3 image55/56`), and the owner photos (Santa's Sleigh `end.jpg`, Frog pano).

Calibration: scores follow the owner's round-1 bar (foam/over-softened = 4, cohesive vapour = 7–8), kept about one point harsher as agreed. Nothing in this set is accepted; the best is a 4.

## What the references ask for

- **Fig 4.16 / slide 37**: domed tops carrying 3–4x smaller billows, flat-ish bases that go wispy (inverted Worley + curl at the base), a real value range: bright lit crust over mid-grey bellies, blue-grey shadow sides, dark undersides. Distant clouds shrink and haze toward the horizon but stay visible as a field with blue between them.
- **Owner photos**: crisp lit crust a few metres thick, darker interior showing through gaps, low-density connective tissue binding the lobes into one body.
- **image55 (HFW sunset)**: backlit deck with glowing thin parts, dark cores, lobed silhouettes; the rim light is on the crust, not drawn as an outline around every shape.

## Pairs

Camera, horizon height, sun and ground shading match within four pairs. Within each pair the cloud shapes differ by one shape-stage change, which I read as **billows vs plates**: one version erodes into round billows, the other into stacked flat slabs with horizontal striations.

## Pair verdicts

- **A: h37 over h35.** Billows give domed tops and a varied skyline; the plate version turns the same masses into stacked striated slabs that read as batting. The billow version still has no hierarchy, so the win is modest (4 vs 3).
- **B: h66 over h71.** Same reason; h66 is the best frame in the set because its sky-wide layout (big overhead, smaller toward the horizon, satellites) is the closest to Fig 4.16.
- **C: h13 over h88.** Rim light on lobed shapes beats rim light on cut-paper slabs; h13's edges are the only crisp edges in the set. Both share the opaque bodies and the straight base line.
- **D: h15 marginally over h11.** h15 has edge information and some crevice shading, which is the direction the owner's crust rule wants; h11 is a cohesive but featureless foam. h15 pays for it with sparkle along the horizon gap and a visible ceiling plane. Neither is near acceptable (3/3).

Direction for the build: keep the billow erosion (h37/h66/h13 side) everywhere; the plate variant should not ship.

## Three biggest gaps to HZD

1. **Lighting and material.** No value range. Noon frames are front-lit cotton: no dark bellies, no blue-grey shadow flanks, no crevice darkening, so lobes never read as separate volumes and the material reads as foam. At sunset the rim light is a uniform thin outline around every shape and the bodies are one flat opaque grey with no in-scatter glow through thin parts. HZD's look is a bright lit crust over a darker interior; this set has the crust brightness everywhere and the interior nowhere. The owner's round-1 hypothesis (density-to-opacity mapping, extinction, sharpening pow) is the first thing to test; this is a rendering gap before it is a shape gap.
2. **Shape hierarchy and bases.** Erosion is single-scale popcorn; there is no 3–4x step from dome to billow to ripple. Bases are either cut dead flat at the layer floor (h13, visible as a straight line) or lumpy; nothing goes wispy (Fig 4.15, slide 37's inverted Worley + curl). Tops in the aerial views truncate at a ceiling plane. The plate variant adds horizontal striations that nothing in HZD or the photos has.
3. **Sky-wide depth and the horizon.** The layer reads as one thin sheet at one altitude with no towering or type variety across the sky. Aerial perspective turns into a solid white band that erases the distant field early (h35/h37), the low-horizon frames show pale streaks on the ground just below the horizon (ring artefact, h66/h71), and near-camera cloud blurs to mush (h11/h15). Edge speckle (ray-march dither visible as sparkle) is on every frame and is a secondary polish gap.
